TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 800d221b presents three training pairs and one test case. The
grids are 16x16, 10x10, and 20x20 in training and 30x30 in the
test; the output always keeps the input size. Each scene draws a
wire network on a uniform background using exactly three
foreground colors. One color forms the wire network itself. The
other two are content colors, mixed together inside the small
blocks that the wires join - every block holds cells of both
colors, and the blocks sit in the corners and along the edges of
the scene. The output repaints every wire cell with the majority
content color of the block its branch of the network reaches,
except at the crossing point of the network: there a three-by-three
ring keeps the wire color, and the single cell at its center takes
the color of the winning side.

Two properties make the task hard. First, the four colors permute
freely across pairs - background, wire, and the two content colors
are different in every grid - so every role must be derived from
structure at solve time. Second, the center verdict at the crossing
is decided by a cell-level tally, not a branch-level one: in the
third training pair the losing color owns three branches against
two, yet the winning color takes the center because its branches
touch the ring at two separate places each.

RULE: junction_hub

The glass-box rule is a five-stage procedure whose parameters are
all read off the input grid at solve time:

1. ROLE RECOGNITION (jh_centers_/5, jh_solid_/4)
   The most common color is the background. Among the exactly
   three foreground colors, the wire color is the only one owning
   exactly one cell whose full three-by-three neighborhood holds
   that same color. That cell is the hub - the crossing point of
   the network. The two content colors have either no such solid
   window (their blocks are small and mixed) or many (their solid
   regions are large).

2. RING PRESERVATION
   The eight cells around the hub form the ring. The hub and ring
   are removed from the wire set; the ring cells keep the wire
   color in the output.

3. BRANCH EXTRACTION (jh_parts_/4, jh_flood_/4, jh_step_/4)
   The remaining wire cells split into four-connected branches -
   the arms of the network that lead away from the crossing
   toward the content blocks.

4. BRANCH REPAINTING (jh_branch_color_/6, jh_meets_/2,
   jh_majority_/2, jh_cells_/3)
   The content cells form four-connected blocks of mixed colors.
   Every branch collects the blocks it is four-adjacent to and is
   repainted with the majority color over all cells of those
   blocks.

5. HUB VERDICT
   Every branch cell that is four-adjacent to a ring cell casts
   one vote for its branch's assigned color, and the hub takes
   the majority color of those votes.

The discriminating observation is the cell-level tally at the
hub. Counting distinct branches gives the wrong answer on the
third training pair, where the color 5 branches outnumber the
color 6 branches three to two: the two 6 branches each wrap onto
the ring at two separate places and win the vote four cells to
three. A branch that touches the ring twice carries double
weight, and only the touching-cell tally reads all three training
pairs and the test grid consistently.

VERIFICATION

The complete rule was written in plain English covering all three
training pairs and the test case before any code, following the
proven wave method: all four grids were dumped to text, every
block majority, branch attachment, and ring touchpoint was mapped
by hand, and a Python prototype passed all three training pairs

the single-task test and the full regression on the first

exactly, and every previously solved task still passes. Induction

exactly four distinct colors) followed by transformation of every
