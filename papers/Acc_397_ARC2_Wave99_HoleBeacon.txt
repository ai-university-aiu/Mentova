TASK DESCRIPTION

Task de809cff presents two training pairs and one test case. The
grids are 20x20 in training and 30x30 in the test; the output
always keeps the input size. Each input holds two large
rectilinear regions of two distinct colors on a zero background -
3 and 4 in the first pair, 1 and 2 in the second, 1 and 6 in the
test - disturbed by three kinds of single-cell noise: background
holes punched inside the regions, wrong-colored cells embedded
inside the regions, and colored strays scattered over the
background. The output cleans all three kinds of noise and marks
every hole with a 3x3 beacon whose ring takes the RIVAL region's
color and whose center is painted 8.

Two properties make the task hard. First, the region boundaries
must be reconstructed from noisy evidence: a wrong-colored cell
one step inside a straight border flips to the majority side, a
region cell at a convex corner survives with only three same-color
neighbors, and a colored bump sticking out of a straight edge is
erased with the same three - so simple connectivity and simple
neighbor counts both misclassify. Second, on the test grid two
stray cells sit exactly two steps from a background cell near the
region border, and a single majority vote over raw colors stamps a
spurious beacon there - two locally measured votes must resolve
differently depending on which neighbors are themselves noise.

RULE: hole_beacon

The glass-box rule is a six-stage procedure whose parameters are
all read off the input grid at solve time:

1. PALETTE READING (hb_colors_/3)
   The grid's palette is exactly background zero plus two region
   colors A and B, recovered by sorting the flattened cell list.

2. FIRST VOTE PASS (hb_label_pass_/5, hb_vote_/7, hb_probe_/2)
   Every cell is labeled A, B, or background by a majority vote
   over the eight cells of its orthogonal cross neighborhood -
   distances one and two up, down, left, and right, in-bounds
   probes only - voting on the raw input colors. Ties are broken
   by the cell's own input color when it is among the tied
   leaders, and by background otherwise.

3. SECOND VOTE PASS
   The identical vote runs again, over the first pass's labels
   instead of the raw colors. Strays that the first pass silenced
   to background now stay silent in every neighbor's vote.

4. BASE OUTPUT
   Cells labeled background become 0 - strays erased. Cells
   labeled with a region become that region's color -
   wrong-colored cells repaired, clean cells kept. The final
   label grid is itself the base output.

5. BEACON RINGS (hb_centers_/3, hb_ring_/5, hb_paint_/4)
   Every input cell that is background zero yet carries a region
   label is a hole in that region. Each hole becomes a beacon: a
   3x3 stamp centered on the hole whose ring takes the rival
   region's color. Stamps clip at the grid edges and paint over
   anything beneath them, background included.

6. BEACON CENTERS (hb_center_/4)
   Every beacon center is painted 8 after all rings are stamped,
   so overlapping beacons keep every center visible.

The discriminating observation is the second vote pass. On the
test grid, a background cell near the region border sees one
region neighbor at distance one and three noise cells - two strays
and one kept edge cell - within its cross neighborhood, and the
raw-color vote tips four to three toward the region, stamping a
spurious beacon. A distance-weighted vote repairs the test but
breaks both training pairs at region corners. The two-pass label
vote repairs all three grids: after pass one the strays already
carry background labels, the vote falls to background, and no
beacon appears.

VERIFICATION

The complete rule was written in plain English covering both
training pairs and the test case before any code, following the
proven wave method: all six grids were dumped to text, and every
changed cell - every erased stray, every repaired cell, every
beacon ring and center, including overlapping beacons whose
centers survive inside each other's rings - was accounted for by
hand in a full cell-level diff. A Python prototype confirmed the
rule, exposing the spurious-beacon vote that the second pass
resolves. The Prolog port then passed the single-task test and
the full regression on the first compile: induction returns
hole_beacon, the test output matches exactly, and every
previously solved task still passes. Induction is pure
verification: a cheap pre-filter (same-size output, input palette
of exactly zero plus two colors, output containing 8) followed by
transformation of every training pair.
