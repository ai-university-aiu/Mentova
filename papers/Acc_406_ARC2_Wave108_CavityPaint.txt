TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 3a25b0d8 presents two training pairs and one test case, on
inputs of 22x22 and 30x30 with outputs of 11x11, 13x7, and 18x13.
Every input holds exactly two foreground shapes on a uniform
majority-color background, and the two shapes are the same figure
drawn twice.

One drawing is a hollow silhouette: a closed skeleton drawn
entirely in a single color, whose enclosed background pockets are
the figure's cavities. The other drawing is a multi-color
template: the same figure redrawn at a different size and with a
visible warp, its cavities already filled with paint colors. The
background is 8, 1, and 0 across the three grids; the silhouette
color is 1, 2, and 4 - every role is read off each grid, not
memorized.

The output crops to the silhouette's bounding box. Silhouette
cells keep the silhouette color. Every enclosed cavity is painted
with the color of its counterpart region in the template. Open
background - background connected to the bounding-box border -
stays background.

The delicate part is deciding which template region is the
counterpart of which cavity. The template is warped: its regions
sit on different rows and columns than the silhouette's cavities,
its region shapes differ cell for cell, and its region sizes
differ. In the first training pair the silhouette's middle cavity
starts one row below its two flanking cavities, while the
template's matching paint region starts on the same row as its
flankers - so matching by top-to-bottom order assigns the wrong
color, and matching by absolute position or by size fails the
same way. The invariant that survives the warp is position
proportional to the bounding box: each region's centroid,
normalized to its own shape's bounding box, lands nearly on top of
its counterpart's normalized centroid. This normalized-centroid
pairing is the discriminating observation of the wave - it lands
every color on every cavity across both training pairs and all
nine cavities of the test.

RULE: cavity_paint

The glass-box rule is a six-stage procedure whose parameters are
all read off the input grid at solve time:

1. BACKGROUND (arc2_bg_color_/2)
   The majority color is the background. Everything else is
   foreground.

2. TWO-SHAPE SPLIT (cvp_split_/5, cvp_fg_cells_/3, cvp_comps_/3,
   cvp_flood_/5, cvp_colors_/3)
   The foreground splits into 8-connected components. Exactly two
   must exist: the component drawn in a single color is the mask
   (the silhouette), and the component drawn in two or more
   colors is the template.

3. CROP (cvp_bbox_/5)
   The output frame is the mask's bounding box. Every mask cell
   keeps the mask color.

4. CAVITY DETECTION (cvp_hole_comps_/4, cvp_on_border_/3)
   The background inside the bounding box is flooded
   4-connectedly from the border cells. Background the flood
   never reaches is enclosed - these cells are the cavities,
   grouped into 4-connected regions.

5. TEMPLATE REGIONS (cvp_templ_regions_/4)
   Template cells whose color is neither the background nor the
   mask color are the paint; they are grouped per color into
   4-connected regions.

6. DYEING (cvp_dye_/9, cvp_norm_centroid_/7, cvp_render_/7)
   Every cavity and every template region gets a centroid
   normalized to its own shape's bounding box. Each cavity is
   painted with the color of the template region whose normalized
   centroid lies nearest in squared euclidean distance, and the
   output grid is rendered: mask color on mask cells, paint on
   cavity cells, background elsewhere.
