TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 9aaea919 presents three training pairs and one test case. All
grids are 30x30 and the output always keeps the input size. Each
input shows towers of diamond glyphs standing on a uniform
background. A glyph is a three-row, five-column stamp: the middle
row is solid in the glyph color, and the top and bottom rows carry
the color over the middle three cells with background corners. The
glyphs stack upward from the bottom of the grid, one background
row between glyphs, so consecutive centers sit four rows apart,
and every glyph of a tower shares one color. The bottom grid row
carries five-wide marker bars aligned exactly under specific
towers: bars of color 2 and bars of color 3, with the remaining
towers unmarked.

In the output the markers have done their work and vanished. Every
tower under a color-2 bar keeps its exact height but is repainted
gray with color 5. Every tower under a color-3 bar has grown taller
in its own color. Unmarked towers are untouched, and the bottom row
is clean background.

The heart of the task is the growth amount. Across the three
training pairs the receiver towers grow by two, one, and three
glyphs - and the donor towers under the color-2 bars stand exactly
two, one, and three glyphs tall. The donor's height is the donation.
The test grid then raises the stakes: it carries two color-2 bars
over two one-glyph towers and one color-3 bar over a one-glyph
tower, and the expected output grows the receiver by exactly two
glyphs - the sum of both donor heights. Only the summed-donation
reading covers all four grids.

RULE: tower_donate

The glass-box rule is a five-stage procedure whose parameters are
all read off the input grid at solve time:

1. BAR READING (td_background_/2, td_bars_/3, td_row_runs_/4,
   td_take_run_/5)
   The most frequent color is the background. The bottom grid row
   is scanned into maximal non-background runs; every run must be
   exactly five cells wide and colored 2 (donor) or 3 (receiver).

2. GLYPH DETECTION (td_glyphs_/3, td_glyph_/5)
   Every position where the full thirteen-cell diamond pattern
   holds - solid five-wide middle row, three-wide top and bottom
   arcs, background corners - is collected as a glyph with its
   center row, window start column, and color.

3. TOWER GROUPING (td_towers_/2)
   Glyphs sharing a five-wide column window form a tower; every
   glyph of a tower must share one color, and every marker bar
   must sit exactly under a tower's window.

4. DONATION (td_gray_tower_/3, td_paint_glyph_/5)
   The donated total is the sum of the glyph counts of all donor
   towers. Every donor tower keeps its glyphs but is repainted
   gray with color 5.

5. GROWTH AND CLEANUP (td_grow_tower_/4, td_erase_bar_/5,
   td_set_bg_/5)
   Every receiver tower grows by the donated total: the new glyphs
   stack on top in the tower's own color, each center four rows
   above the previous one. The marker bars are erased to
   background and unmarked towers stay untouched.

The discriminating observation is the donated-sum bookkeeping. The
three training growths (+2, +1, +3) equal the single donor heights
(2, 1, 3) pair by pair, which alone would also fit a rule reading
the donor as a mere gauge; but the test grid carries two donor bars
whose one-glyph towers together grow the receiver by exactly two
glyphs, so the donations must be summed across all donors.
