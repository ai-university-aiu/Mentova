TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 64efde09 presents two training pairs and one test case. The
grids are 26x29 and 21x27 in training and 29x29 in test, all over
background 8. Each input holds two to four rectangular motifs, each
two cells wide and seven or nine cells long, plus exactly three
singleton marker cells sitting on the grid edges. The motif
palettes differ across grids - colors 4, 2, and 3 in the first
training input, colors 4, 2, and 1 in the second, and colors 1, 4,
2, and 3 in the test - but every motif carries a run of color 2
along exactly one of its long sides. The output merges the input
with straight shadow rays cast from every motif.

RULE: motif_shadow

The glass-box rule is a five-stage procedure whose parameters are
all read off the input grid at solve time:

1. SEGMENTATION (tw_background_/2, tw_components_/2, ms_classify_/3)
   The background is the most frequent color. The 8-connected
   foreground components split into motif rectangles - two cells
   wide, at least four cells long, bounding box completely filled,
   color-2 cells on exactly one long side - and singleton markers.

2. ANCHOR ENDS (ms_motif_/2)
   Each motif's anchor end is the long-axis end farther from its
   color-2 run.

3. MARKER LESSONS (ms_lesson_/7)
   Each marker sits on a grid edge. A marker on the left or right
   edge aligns with the vertical motif whose row span holds the
   marker row; a marker on the top or bottom edge aligns with the
   horizontal motif whose column span holds the marker column. The
   marker teaches one lesson - the pair of its axis offset from the
   aligned motif's anchor end and its own color - and casts one
   polarity vote by comparing its side of the motif with the
   motif's 2-run side.

4. POLARITY (sort/2 over the votes)
   All polarity votes must agree, fixing whether rays exit on the
   same cross side as the 2-run or on the opposite side. The
   polarity is grid-wide and applies to every motif.

5. RAY CASTING (ms_ray_/6, ms_line_cell_/11, ms_row_/5)
   Every motif casts one full ray per lesson, perpendicular to its
   long axis at the lesson offset from its own anchor end, from its
   exit side out to the grid edge, painting only background cells.
   Motif cells, marker cells, and everything else stay unchanged.

INDUCTION

The induce clause dispatches early, directly after stripe_cycle.
Its pre-filter requires the first training pair to preserve the
grid dimensions, a sparse foreground of 10 to 200 cells, at least
two motif rectangles that all parse under ms_motif_/2, and exactly
three singleton markers. The clause then verifies every training
pair with a forall over the transform, so induction is pure
verification.

KEY INSIGHT

The training pairs alone underdetermine the exit direction. In the
first training grid every motif exits on the same side as its
color-2 run, while in the second training grid and in the test grid
every motif exits on the opposite side, and no structural feature
of the motifs distinguishes the two cases - the motif palettes
themselves differ across grids. The markers resolve it: each marker
lies on the ray of its own color, so its side of the aligned motif
reveals the grid's polarity, turning an apparent per-grid special
case into one learned parameter. The same markers also carry the
offset-to-color dictionary, so the entire transformation - which
lines, which colors, which direction - is read off the input grid
at solve time.

VERIFICATION

Both training pairs and the test case pass in SWI-Prolog under
arc2_induce_rule/2 followed by arc2_transform/3. A full regression
of all 80 locally verified tasks (79 prior plus 64efde09) still
induces and passes with zero regressions. Implementation: nine
ms_* helper predicates and the motif_shadow transform at the end of
arc_benchmark_2.pl, with one explanatory comment above every code
line. The rule passed the single-task test and the full regression
on the first compile.

METHODOLOGY

Pure induction from training examples only. No pretraining on the
ARC-AGI-2 corpus. Named glass-box rules for every solved task.
