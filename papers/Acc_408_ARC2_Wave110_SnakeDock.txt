TASK DESCRIPTION

Task a25697e4 presents three training pairs on 18x25 grids and one
test case on a 30x30 grid. Every scene holds two kinds of objects
on a majority-color background (1 in training, 4 in the test -
every role is read off each grid, not memorized).

The first kind is a SNAKE: a single 4-connected piece made of
exactly two single-color segments touching orthogonally - for
example a six-cell segment of 3s joined to an eight-cell segment
of 4s, or a six-cell segment of 5s joined to a five-cell segment
of 7s.

The second kind is a CONTAINER: one or two nearby wall components
of a single color arranged so that the background cells inside
the cluster's bounding box form a pocket - the SOCKET - open to
the outside through a mouth. Each training grid holds one snake
and one container; the test grid holds two snakes and two
containers at once.

In the output, every snake has left its original position and
docked into its container: one segment fills the socket exactly,
and the other segment trails outside through the mouth on what
were background cells. The docking is rigid - the whole snake is
carried by a single grid isometry (identity, a rotation, or a
reflection) plus a translation, chosen so that the fitting
segment matches the socket shape cell for cell.

The discriminating observation of the wave is the COLOR LAW. Two
of the training grids share the same snake pixel for pixel, and
only the container differs - it is mirrored top to bottom. Yet
one output paints the socket with the fitting segment's own color
while the other paints it with the partner segment's color. The
only property that predicts the choice, across all five docking
events of the task, is the parity of the docking isometry: after
a proper rotation (identity, 90, 180, or 270 degrees) each
segment keeps its own color; after a reflection the two segment
colors are exchanged - a snake that must be flipped over to fit
shows its underside. The five events split as transpose
(reflection, colors swapped), rotation by 90 degrees
counter-clockwise (proper, colors kept), pure translation
(proper, colors kept), rotation by 90 degrees clockwise (proper,
colors kept), and vertical flip (reflection, colors swapped) -
five of five.

RULE: snake_dock

The glass-box rule is a six-stage procedure whose parameters are
all read off the input grid at solve time:

1. SCENE ROLES (arc2_bg_color_/2, snd_components_/3)
   The majority color is the background; the per-color
   4-connected foreground components are gathered.

2. SNAKES AND WALLS (snd_split_snakes_/3, snd_adjacent_/2)
   Two components of different colors touching orthogonally form
   a snake; every component touching nothing is a container
   wall. Each component may belong to at most one snake.

3. CONTAINERS (snd_clusters_/2, snd_near_same_/2, snd_socket_/4)
   Same-color wall components lying within Chebyshev distance
   two are clustered into containers - a container may be drawn
   as two separate walls with the mouth between them. The socket
   is the set of background cells inside the cluster's bounding
   box, and container and snake counts must agree.

4. DOCKING SEARCH (snd_match_all_/5, snd_match_one_/6,
   snd_map_cells_/3, snd_norm_/3)
   For each socket in discovery order, the first valid docking
   is taken in a fixed deterministic order: snakes in discovery
   order, each snake's two segments in turn, and the eight grid
   isometries in the order identity, three rotations, four
   reflections. A candidate segment must have exactly as many
   cells as the socket, its transformed shape must match the
   socket exactly, and the partner segment, carried by the same
   rigid map, must land entirely on in-grid background cells
   without overlapping the socket.

5. PAINTING (snd_erase_snakes_/4, snd_paint_/4)
   Every snake is erased from its original position; the socket
   is filled and the trail is laid through the mouth.

6. COLOR LAW (snd_iso_parity_/2)
   After a proper rotation each segment keeps its own color;
   after a reflection the socket takes the partner segment's
   color and the trail takes the fitting segment's color - the
   flipped snake shows its underside.

VERIFICATION

The complete rule was written in plain English covering all three
training pairs and the test case before any code, following the
proven wave method: all eight grids were dumped to text, the
exact cell coordinates of every segment, socket, and trail were
extracted, and every one of the five docking events was verified
by hand - fitting segment, isometry, translation offset, trail
position, and painted colors all match cell for cell. A Python
prototype then confirmed the algorithm and its deterministic
search order on all four grids, passing training and test on its
first run. The integrated Prolog rule passed the single-task test
and the full regression on its first compile: induction returns
snake_dock, the test output matches exactly, and every previously
solved task still passes. Induction is pure verification: a cheap
pre-filter (equal input and output dimensions, identical
background, and an unchanged foreground cell count - the docked
snake keeps every cell) followed by exact transformation of every
training pair.

A lesson of independent interest: when identical inputs produce
differently colored outputs, the discriminator hides in the part
of the scene that DID change. Here the mirrored container forces
a mirrored docking, and the parity of the isometry - rotation
versus reflection - is the semantic bit. Treat rigid placements
as isometry plus translation and test the parity as a
first-class feature: reflections can carry meaning, exactly as a
flipped physical tile shows its underside.
