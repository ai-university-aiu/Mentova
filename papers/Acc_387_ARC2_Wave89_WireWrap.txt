TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 88bcf3b4 presents five training pairs and one test case. All
grids keep their size, ranging from 7x7 up to 22x22. Each grid
holds one or more three-color systems on a uniform background: a
straight orthogonal line segment, a crumpled meandering wire
attached orthogonally to one endpoint of the line, and a separate
marker shape - a single cell, a short bar, a zigzag, or a staircase
blob. The output keeps the line and the marker exactly where they
are, erases the crumpled wire, and re-lays it as a clean path that
leaves the line end, runs diagonally to the marker, bends snugly
around the marker's near face, and departs on the mirrored
diagonal. The test grid doubles the difficulty by holding two
independent systems - six foreground colors - in one scene.

RULE: wire_wrap

The glass-box rule is a four-stage procedure whose parameters are
all read off the input grid at solve time:

1. SYSTEM DISCOVERY (tw_background_/2, ww_groups_/2,
   ww_connected_/1, ww_straight_/1, ww_orth_/2, ww_nearest_/3)
   The majority color is the background, and every remaining color
   must form exactly one 8-connected component. A component is a
   line when it is a straight orthogonal segment of at least three
   cells, a wire when it is crumpled and one of its cells touches a
   line endpoint orthogonally, and a marker otherwise. Line colors
   and wire colors must pair one to one, and each system takes the
   marker nearest to its attachment endpoint by Chebyshev distance.

2. CLAMPED DIAGONAL APPROACH (ww_sign_/3, ww_approach_/10)
   The approach diagonal takes, per axis, the sign of the marker
   centroid minus the line endpoint; a zero component is tie-broken
   by the side where the input wire's mass lies. From the input
   attachment cell the path steps along that diagonal, each axis
   clamped so it never passes the lane one cell outside the
   marker's bounding box, until the path is 8-adjacent to the
   marker.

3. NEAR-FACE HUG (ww_ctype_/7, ww_face_/9)
   The contact is classified as a row contact (a marker cell ahead
   along the row axis, directly or across the diagonal) or a column
   contact (a marker cell aside along the column axis). A row
   contact hugs the marker's lateral face: for each marker row from
   the contact side to the far side, one hug cell sits one column
   beyond that row's near edge. A column contact hugs the
   longitudinal face symmetrically per column. Because the hug
   follows each row's or column's own near edge, zigzag and
   staircase markers are hugged step by step with no special cases.

4. MIRRORED DEPARTURE AND TRUNCATION (ww_ray_/6,
   ww_ingrid_prefix_/4, tt_render_/5)
   From the last hug cell the path departs on the 45-degree ray
   with the lateral component of the approach diagonal reversed.
   The chained path - approach, hug, departure - is cut at the
   first off-grid cell and then truncated to the input wire's exact
   cell count. The output paints the untouched lines and markers
   plus the re-laid wires on a fresh background canvas.

INDUCTION

The induce clause dispatches early, directly after band_jigsaw. Its
pre-filter requires the first training pair to preserve both grid
dimensions, requires 6 to 250 foreground cells, and requires the
distinct foreground color count to be a positive multiple of three
of at most nine, since each system holds exactly a line, a wire,
and a marker. The clause then verifies every training pair with a
forall over the transform, so induction is pure verification.

KEY INSIGHT

Hand-tracing all twelve grids before coding produced the
discriminating observation: the wire's length is NOT always
conserved. Two of the five training pairs lose wire cells because
the re-laid path hits the grid edge before the length is spent,
which falsifies every rigid-transform, rotation, and
length-preserving reading of the task. The wire is not moved - it
is recomputed as pure geometry, and the input wire contributes only
its color, its cell count, its attachment cell, and a tie-break
side. Once the four phases were written down in plain English and
checked cell by cell against all seven line-wire-marker systems
(five training, two in the test), the implementation passed
everything on the first run.

VERIFICATION

All five training pairs and the test case pass in SWI-Prolog under
arc2_induce_rule/2 followed by arc2_transform/3. A full regression
of all 85 locally verified tasks (84 prior plus 88bcf3b4) still
induces and passes with zero regressions. Implementation: eleven
ww_* helper predicates and the wire_wrap transform at the end of
arc_benchmark_2.pl, with one explanatory comment above every code
line. The rule passed all five training pairs and the test case on
the first prototype run and the full regression on the first
compile.

METHODOLOGY

Pure induction from training examples only. No pretraining on the
ARC-AGI-2 corpus. Named glass-box rules for every solved task.
