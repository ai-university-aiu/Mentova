TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 221dfab4 presents two training pairs and one test case. Both
training grids are 29x22 and the test grid is 25x25. One edge row of
each input carries a horizontal bar of color 4: the bottom row in
both training inputs but the top row in the test input. The bar's
columns are the stripe columns. The rest of the grid scatters
one-color shapes over a majority background - background 1 with
color-8 shapes in the first training pair, background 8 with color-2
shapes in the second, and background 8 with color-6 shapes in the
test. The output repaints every row by a palette that repeats every
six rows, anchored at the marker bar, while leaving everything
outside the stripe columns untouched except on the overlay rows.

RULE: stripe_cycle

The glass-box rule is a three-stage repaint procedure with no
learned parameters - the whole transformation is determined by the
grid:

1. BACKGROUND (tw_background_/2)
   The background is the most frequent color in the grid. It is
   counted, not assumed, because the grids use two different
   backgrounds (1 and 8).

2. MARKER BAR (st_marker_/3)
   The marker row is whichever of the top row and the bottom row
   holds cells of color 4, and the columns of those cells are the
   stripe columns.

3. SIX-ROW PALETTE (st_row_/5, st_cell_/6)
   Every row is rebuilt under the palette entry for its cyclic
   distance D, the absolute row difference from the marker row
   modulo six:
   - D of 0 or 2: the stripe columns take the marker color 4;
     every other cell keeps its input value.
   - Odd D (1, 3, or 5): the stripe columns take the background
     color; every other cell keeps its input value.
   - D of 4: the stripe columns take the overlay color 3, and every
     non-background cell of the row outside the stripes is also
     overlaid with 3.
   Nothing else in the grid moves.

INDUCTION

The induce clause dispatches early, directly after twin_lift. Its
pre-filter requires the first training pair to preserve the grid
dimensions, the first input to carry a color-4 marker bar of at
least two cells in its top or bottom row, and color 4 to appear
nowhere else in that input. The clause then verifies every training
pair with a forall over the transform, so induction is pure
verification.

KEY INSIGHT

The decisive check was the palette anchor. Both training grids put
the marker bar in the bottom row at row 28, and 28 mod 6 equals 4,
so a palette anchored at row zero - stripe color 3 on rows 0, 6,
12, ... and stripe color 4 on rows 2, 4, 8, 10, ... - reproduces
both training outputs perfectly. It still fails the hidden test:
the test grid moves the bar to the top row, where a row-zero anchor
would paint the marker bar itself green. Anchoring the cycle at the
marker row survives both placements and reproduces all three
outputs. When two anchor readings tie on the training pairs, the
test input often discriminates; reading every visible grid before
coding avoided a wasted wave.

VERIFICATION

Both training pairs and the test case pass in SWI-Prolog under
arc2_induce_rule/2 followed by arc2_transform/3. A full regression
of all 79 locally verified tasks (78 prior plus 221dfab4) still
induces and passes with zero regressions. Implementation: three
st_* helper predicates and the stripe_cycle transform at the end of
arc_benchmark_2.pl, with one explanatory comment above every code
line. The rule passed the single-task test and the full regression
on the first compile.

METHODOLOGY

Pure induction from training examples only. No pretraining on the
ARC-AGI-2 corpus. Named glass-box rules for every solved task.
