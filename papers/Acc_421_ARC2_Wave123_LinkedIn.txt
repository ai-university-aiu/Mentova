The glass-box rule I call nested_contour:

Step 1: the background is the most common color; split the grid into regions - each background area as a four-connected component, each stretch of line color as an eight-connected component.

Step 2: build a containment tree by breadth-first search outward from the border background (the outer sea, depth zero), giving each region a depth and a parent so loops and areas alternate down the tree.

Step 3: render each region recursively - a leaf becomes a single cell of its color (the innermost dot), and an internal region renders its children, arranges them, and wraps the result in a one-cell frame of its own color.

Step 4: arrange siblings by clustering their innermost-dot anchors into row and column bands and snapping each block onto the shared anchor line, then pad any grid edge a lone dot touches with background.

The discriminating detail is that the output keeps only the nesting topology - who is inside whom, and how deep - and rebuilds the picture from that tree alone. Every original size and coordinate is thrown away, so sibling nests must be re-registered to one another by their innermost dots. That anchor alignment is the only thing that decides where two shrunk nests sit once their old positions are gone.
