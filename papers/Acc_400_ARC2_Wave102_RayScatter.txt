TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 4a21e3da presents three training pairs and one test case. The
training grids are 18x18 and the test grid is 20x20; the output
always keeps the input size. Each input shows exactly three colors:
a uniform background (1), one connected blob of a single color (7)
sitting near the middle of the grid, and one or two lone marker
cells (2) resting on the grid border - on the top edge, the bottom
edge, or the right edge across the four grids. In the output the
blob has been blasted apart. Each marker has fired a ray into the
grid along its own row or column, reaching exactly as far as the
farthest blob cell on that line; the background cells along the ray
are painted the marker color, while the blob cells lying on the
line keep their color and stay exactly where they were. Every other
blob cell has flown away: the blob's side pieces have translated
rigidly into the corners of the grid, each landing flush against
two walls, preserving its shape cell for cell.

Two properties make the task hard. First, the movement rule mixes
two different corner axes: across a ray a piece moves away from the
line to the wall on its own side, but along a single ray the piece
moves toward the edge holding the marker - both side pieces of the
one-ray grids fly to the marker's end of the grid, not away from
it. Second, one piece of the two-ray training pair simply vanishes:
the bottom-left quadrant is deleted from the scene, and nothing in
the local geometry of that piece explains why.

RULE: ray_scatter

The glass-box rule is a five-stage procedure whose parameters are
all read off the input grid at solve time:

1. SCENE READING (rs_scene_/6)
   The three colors are ranked by frequency: the most frequent is
   the background, the next is the blob, and the rarest - at most
   two cells - is the marker color. Marker cells must sit on the
   grid border.

2. RAY FIRING (rs_rays_/4, rs_ray_/4, rs_ray_far_/3)
   Each marker fires a ray along its own row or column - downward
   from the top edge, upward from the bottom edge, rightward from
   the left edge, leftward from the right edge - stopping at the
   farthest blob cell on that line.

3. RAY PAINTING (rs_draw_ray_/6, rs_ray_span_/3, rs_ray_cell_/7)
   Every line position between the marker and the stopping point is
   painted: blob cells keep the blob color and stay in place,
   background cells take the marker color.

4. PIECE SPLITTING (rs_on_ray_/2, rs_groups_/3, rs_key_/4,
   rs_vside_/3, rs_hside_/3)
   Blob cells on a ray line are anchored. The remaining blob cells
   split into side pieces: with two rays, the four quadrants around
   the crossing point; with one ray, the two sides of the line,
   with the along-ray side given by the marker's own edge.

5. CORNER PLACEMENT (rs_marker_edges_/4, rs_place_/7,
   rs_offsets_/7, rs_shift_put_/6)
   Every piece translates rigidly so its bounding box lands flush
   in its destination corner. A piece whose destination corner
   touches no edge holding a marker is deleted from the scene
   entirely.

The discriminating observation is the deleted quadrant. In the
two-ray training pair the markers sit on the top edge and the right
edge; the top-left, top-right, and bottom-right pieces all land in
corners touching a marker edge and survive, but the bottom-left
corner touches neither marked edge, and its six-cell piece vanishes
from the output. Only the corner-touches-a-marker-edge test
explains why three sibling pieces survive while the fourth is
swallowed.

VERIFICATION

training pairs and the test case before any code, following the
proven wave method: all eight grids were dumped to text and every
changed cell - every painted ray cell, every anchored on-line blob
cell, every translated piece cell, and every deleted cell - was

matches exactly, and every previously solved task still passes.
Induction is pure verification: a cheap pre-filter (same-size
output, exactly three colors with the rarest on the border, every
ray line crossing the blob) followed by transformation of every
