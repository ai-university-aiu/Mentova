TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 2c181942 presents three training pairs and one test case. The
grids are 23x20, 18x21, and 20x25 in training and 24x26 in the
test; the output always keeps the input size. Each scene shows one
hub piece - an eight-cell ring in a four-by-four bounding box whose
four sides carry two centered cells each, every side in its own
color - plus loose single-color shapes scattered on the background.
The output rebuilds the scene from a fresh background canvas: the
hub stays exactly where it was, every shape whose color matches a
hub side is rotated and parked flush against that side, and every
shape whose color matches no side vanishes.

Two properties make the task hard. First, the docking orientation
is a genuine unknown: the same scene docks one shape by a clockwise
quarter turn and another by a counterclockwise quarter turn, so no
fixed rotation works. Second, a plain adjacency check admits wrong
fits: in the second training pair the left-docking shape owns a
three-cell side whose two cells would touch the hub edge rows, yet
the correct fit uses its bottom side - only a centered-contact
condition separates the two.

RULE: hub_dock

The glass-box rule is a six-stage procedure whose parameters are
all read off the input grid at solve time:

1. SCENE SEGMENTATION (tw_components_/2, arc2_bg_color_/2)
   The most common color is the background. The foreground cells
   group into eight-connected components. Exactly one component
   mixes four distinct colors: the hub, an eight-cell ring in a
   four-by-four bounding box.

2. SIDE READING (hd_edge_/5)
   Each side of the hub bounding box - top row, bottom row, left
   column, right column - holds exactly two adjacent cells of one
   color, and the four side colors all differ. Each side records
   its color, its direction, and the start of its two edge cells.

3. CANVAS RESET (arc2_sb_blank_/4, hd_put_/3)
   The output starts as an all-background canvas of the input
   size, and the hub is painted back unchanged.

4. ORIENTATION SEARCH (hd_orient_/7, hd_rot_/7, hd_face_/4,
   hd_centered_/2)
   Every other component is a loose single-color shape. For each
   shape whose color owns a hub side, the four quarter-turn
   rotations are tried in order, and the first one wins whose
   bounding-box side facing the hub holds exactly two adjacent
   cells centered along that side: the side length must be even
   and the cells must sit at the two middle positions.

5. FLUSH PLACEMENT (hd_place_/7)
   The rotated shape is parked flush against the hub - its facing
   bounding-box line one cell away from the hub side - and
   translated so its two contact cells sit directly next to the
   two hub edge cells. It is painted in its own color.

6. DROP RULE (hd_dock_/7)
   A shape whose color matches no hub side is dropped, and so is
   any matching shape with no fitting orientation; in the test
   grid the green shape has no green hub side and vanishes.

The discriminating observation is the centered-contact condition.
Requiring the hub-facing side to hold exactly the two centered
cells rejects the full-length sides and the off-center sides that
a plain adjacency check would accept, and it pins both the
rotation and the translation uniquely. The rotations are pure
quarter turns with no reflection, which the first training pair
proves in both directions: the same shape family docks left by a
clockwise turn and right by a counterclockwise turn.

VERIFICATION

The complete rule was written in plain English covering all three
training pairs and the test case before any code, following the
proven wave method: all four grids were dumped to text, every hub
side, every docking orientation, and every contact cell was mapped
by hand, and a Python prototype passed all three training pairs
and the test grid on the first run. The Prolog port then passed
the single-task test and the full regression on the first compile:
induction returns hub_dock, the test output matches exactly, and
every previously solved task still passes. Induction is pure
verification: a cheap pre-filter (same-size output, five to eight
distinct colors) followed by transformation of every training
pair.
