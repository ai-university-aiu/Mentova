TASK DESCRIPTION

Task a32d8b75 presents three training pairs on 20x30 grids and one
test case on a 30x30 grid. Every input carries one or two LEGEND
PANELS beside full-height divider columns of color 6 - each
training grid has a single panel on the left, while the test grid
has a panel on the left AND a panel on the right. The output drops
the panels and the dividers entirely, so the training outputs are
20x24 and the test output is 30x20: only the PLAYING FIELD between
the panels survives.

Each panel is a four-part stamping program:

1. A STAMP GLYPH - a small solid block drawn in exactly two
   colors (3x3 in training, 2x2 in the test).

2. A LAYOUT GLYPH - a larger figure drawn in exactly one color
   (6x5, 6x3, and 5x3 in training; 8x4 and 6x4 in the test).

3. An ANCHOR BOX - a bordered box holding a single 4 whose
   position in a corner of the box interior names the corner of
   the playing field where the painting is anchored flush.

4. A ROTATION BOX - a bordered box holding a two-cell 7-domino
   needle. A vertical needle in the upper half of the box
   commands no rotation; in the lower half, a half turn. A
   horizontal needle in the right half commands a quarter turn
   clockwise; in the left half, three quarter turns.

The output paints the layout glyph onto the field at the anchored
corner, rotated as the needle commands and scaled up by the
stamp's dimensions: every set cell of the layout becomes a full
copy of the stamp with its two colors EXCHANGED, and every clear
cell keeps the field pixels underneath untouched.

The discriminating observation of the wave is that the anchor and
the rotation are a TWO-PART command that must be read jointly.
Across the five panels of the task the pairs are: top-left with
no turn (train 2), top-right with a quarter turn clockwise
(train 3), bottom-right with a half turn (train 1), bottom-right
with no turn (test, left panel), and top-left with a half turn
(test, right panel). The same corner appears with two different
rotations and the same rotation with two different corners, so
any theory reading only one marker collapses on some panel - yet
the pair predicts all five placements exactly.

RULE: fractal_stamp

The glass-box rule is a six-stage procedure whose parameters are
all read off the input grid at solve time:

1. PANELS AND FIELD (frs_divider_cols_/3, frs_panels_/5)
   Every full-height column of color 6 is a divider; each
   divider owns a legend panel on the side toward the nearer
   grid edge, and the playing field is the column range that
   remains. The field is cut out as the output canvas.

2. PANEL ANATOMY (frs_slice_grid_/4)
   Inside a panel, the two all-6 separator rows are found: above
   the first lies the glyph zone, between them the anchor box,
   and below the second the rotation box.

3. GLYPH ROLES (frs_comps_/2, frs_glyphs_/4)
   The glyph zone splits into 8-connected components of
   non-background cells. The component drawn in two colors is
   the stamp; the component drawn in one color is the layout.
   The stamp is cut at its bounding box and its two colors are
   exchanged; the layout becomes a boolean mask over its
   bounding box.

4. ANCHOR (frs_interior_cols_/4, frs_marker_cells_/5)
   The anchor box interior is the box rows with every all-6
   border column trimmed. The single 4's position relative to
   the interior midpoints names the vertical and horizontal
   anchor: top or bottom, left or right.

5. ROTATION (frs_rot_mask_/3)
   The 7-domino's two cells either share a column (a vertical
   needle: upper half means no rotation, lower half a half
   turn) or share a row (a horizontal needle: right half means
   a quarter turn clockwise, left half three quarter turns).
   The layout mask is rotated accordingly.

6. PAINTING (frs_place_/6, frs_overlay_/4)
   The painted region is the mask scaled by the stamp height and
   width, placed flush at the anchored corner of the field.
   Every set mask cell paints a full stamp copy; every clear
   cell keeps the field. With several panels, the programs fold
   over the shared field one after another - the two regions of
   the test grid are disjoint, one per panel.

VERIFICATION

The complete rule was written in plain English covering all three
training pairs and the test case before any code, following the
proven wave method: all eight grids were dumped to text and every
stamped block of every output was accounted for by hand - stamp
colors, layout mask, rotation, and anchor corner all match cell
for cell on the five panels. A Python prototype then confirmed
the algorithm; it caught one real bug - the box interiors were
first taken as fixed columns one through panel-width-minus-two,
which wrongly kept a border column inside the interior and
misread the vertical needle of training grid one as horizontal.
The fix trims every all-6 column from the box rows instead. With
that fix the prototype passed all four grids, and the integrated
Prolog rule passed the single-task test and the full regression
on its first compile: induction returns fractal_stamp, the test
output matches exactly, and every previously solved task still
passes. Induction is pure verification: a cheap pre-filter (equal
heights, a strictly narrower output, and at least one full-height
6-column in the input) followed by exact transformation of every
training pair.

A lesson of independent interest: legend instruction boxes can
carry a two-part placement command whose parts are meaningless
alone - a corner marker and a rotation needle - and a two-cell
domino is the minimal rotation needle, distinguishing all four
quarter-turn commands by its offset from the box center. And
when a legend glyph is painted onto the field, expect the
negative: this task paints every stamp with its two colors
exchanged, just as Wave 110's reflected snakes showed their
undersides.
