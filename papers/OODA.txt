WHY THIS PAPER EXISTS

Mentova's hierarchical planner (the co_hplan pack) puts an observe-orient-decide-
act loop at the middle layer of every plan, between the top goal (Win Game) and the
concrete controls. That middle layer is John Boyd's OODA loop. This paper sets down,
accurately, what the OODA loop actually is - not the cartoon four-box circle, but
Boyd's real diagram with its feedback and its implicit-guidance shortcuts and its
insistence that orientation is the main thing - so that the methodology Mentova
runs is grounded in the source, and so a copy of it can be held in Mentova's
Jacobian-Space workspace as a skill it reasons with. The final section maps the
theory onto co_hplan's middle layer directly.


1. JOHN BOYD: THE MAN BEHIND THE LOOP

John Richard Boyd (1927 to 1997) was a United States Air Force fighter pilot and
one of the most influential, and least conventionally published, military
strategists of the twentieth century. He flew the F-86 Sabre in the Korean War and
became an instructor at the Fighter Weapons School at Nellis Air Force Base, where
he earned the nickname Forty Second Boyd for a standing wager: starting from a
position of disadvantage on an opponent's tail, he could reverse the situation and
win the simulated dogfight within forty seconds. He is said never to have lost the
bet.

His first major contribution was doctrinal. His 1960 Aerial Attack Study
systematized air-combat maneuvering and became the de facto global standard for
fighter tactics. In the early 1960s, with mathematician Thomas Christie, he
developed Energy-Maneuverability theory, which expressed an aircraft's combat
potential in terms of its specific energy (a function of altitude, speed, thrust,
drag, and weight) and its ability to change that energy state through maneuver.
Energy-Maneuverability theory became a world standard for fighter design and shaped
the F-15, the F-16, and the A-10.

Crucially, Boyd published almost no formal academic papers. His mature strategic
thought lives almost entirely in briefings - hand-drawn slides and hours-long oral
presentations he delivered and endlessly revised - collectively titled A Discourse
on Winning and Losing. Its principal components are Destruction and Creation
(September 3, 1976, his single formal written essay), Patterns of Conflict (his
magnum opus briefing, which shaped United States Marine Corps maneuver-warfare
doctrine), Organic Design for Command and Control, The Strategic Game of ? and ?,
Conceptual Spiral (1991), and The Essence of Winning and Losing (1995, revised
January 1996) - the only place Boyd ever actually drew the full OODA loop diagram.
Boyd died on March 9, 1997, and is buried at Arlington National Cemetery.
Understanding that the OODA loop exists as sketches and briefings, not as a peer-
reviewed paper, is essential: nearly every simplified four-box version in
circulation is a secondhand reconstruction that loses what Boyd actually drew.


2. THE FULL OODA LOOP AS BOYD ACTUALLY DREW IT

The popular image is a tidy circle: Observe, then Orient, then Decide, then Act,
then back to Observe. Boyd's actual 1995 to 1996 diagram is far richer, and its
details carry the real theory. It is a network of feed-forward and feedback flows,
not a single ring.

Observe is fed by three external streams entering it: unfolding circumstances,
outside information, and unfolding interaction with the environment. Observation is
not passive data collection; it is coupled to a changing world that the actor is
simultaneously disturbing.

The main forward flow runs Observe to Orient to Decide to Act. Along the way,
observations feed Orient; Orient yields a decision that Boyd labels a hypothesis;
and the decision yields an action that Boyd labels a test.

Feedback loops close the system. The output of Act feeds back to Observe, because
acting changes the unfolding interaction with the environment, which the actor must
then re-observe. There is also a feedback link from Decide back toward Observe, and
the observation window continuously feeds itself. Acting is literally a test: you
act to see what happens, then observe the result.

The two most important and most often-omitted features are the Implicit Guidance
and Control links. Two arrows run directly out of the Orient box: one from Orient
back to Observe, and one from Orient forward to Act, the latter bypassing Decide
entirely. They are labeled Implicit Guidance and Control. They represent the fast,
intuitive path of the experienced operator: orientation directs what the actor even
bothers to observe, and, when a situation matches a well-honed mental pattern,
orientation can drive action directly with no explicit conscious decision. This is
exactly how Forty Second Boyd won - not by deliberating through four sequential
boxes, but by having such deep orientation that observation flowed almost instantly
into action. The explicit Decide step is used mainly when the situation is novel
and no implicit pattern fits.

So the loop is really many nested, simultaneous loops operating at different speeds:
a slow explicit loop through Decide, and a fast implicit loop that shortcuts it.


3. ORIENTATION AS THE SCHWERPUNKT

Boyd repeatedly insisted that Orientation is the most important element of the
entire system - its schwerpunkt, a German military term meaning the focal point,
the center of gravity, the main thing. In his own words, orientation shapes
observation, shapes decision, shapes action, and in turn is shaped by the feedback
and other phenomena coming into the observation window. Orientation is thus both
cause and consequence: it filters what we perceive, frames our options, biases our
actions, and is itself continuously reshaped by the results. The whole loop, Boyd
wrote, is an ongoing many-sided implicit cross-referencing process of projection,
empathy, correlation, and rejection.

Boyd decomposed Orientation into five ingredients, drawn inside the Orient box as
feeding into one another:

 • Genetic heritage - our innate, biological predispositions.

 • Cultural traditions - the values, language, and social frameworks we are raised
   inside.

 • Previous experience - the accumulated personal history we bring to a situation.

 • New information - the fresh sensory input arriving in the moment.

 • Analysis and synthesis - the active cognitive process, at the center, that
   breaks incoming information apart and recombines it into an updated picture of
   reality.

The first three are inherited or slowly formed; the fourth arrives continuously;
and the fifth is the engine that fuses all of them into a working model of the
world. Because orientation governs everything downstream, a competitor with better
orientation can prevail even when materially weaker: clearer, faster, less-distorted
orientation lets you see reality more accurately and act on it before an opponent
has made sense of the same events.


4. TEMPO: OPERATING INSIDE THE ADVERSARY'S OODA LOOP

The strategic payoff of the loop is tempo. Boyd's central operational claim is that
if you can cycle through observe, orient, decide, and act faster and more accurately
than your adversary, you get inside their decision cycle. As he put it, the ability
to operate at a faster tempo than an adversary enables one to fold the adversary
back inside himself so that he can neither appreciate nor keep up with what is going
on.

The goal is not merely raw speed but the deliberate generation of ambiguity,
novelty, and mismatch. By changing the situation faster than the opponent can re-
orient, you make their orientation increasingly wrong; their observations no longer
match reality, their decisions arrive too late, and their actions address a world
that has already moved on. The adversary's own OODA loop breaks down from the
inside; confusion, paralysis, and panic follow.

Boyd analyzed conflict across three interacting dimensions: the physical (forces,
terrain, resources - the aim is to menace and isolate), the mental (perception and
decision - the aim is to generate uncertainty and doubt), and the moral (cohesion,
trust, legitimacy - the aim is to break the bonds that hold the adversary together
while preserving one's own). He regarded the moral level as the most decisive and
the physical as the least: victory ultimately comes from shattering the opponent's
cohesion and will, not merely his equipment.


5. ANALYSIS, SYNTHESIS, AND DESTRUCTION AND CREATION

Orientation's fifth ingredient, analysis and synthesis, is the subject of Boyd's one
formal essay, Destruction and Creation (1976). There Boyd argued that we build
understanding by two complementary acts: analysis (deduction), which takes an
existing mental model apart into its components, a kind of destruction; and
synthesis (induction), which recombines those components, often across domains, into
a new and more useful model, a kind of creation. Learning and adaptation require
both: we must be willing to destroy an obsolete mental model in order to create a
better one.

Boyd grounded this in three deep results. Godel's incompleteness theorems: a
sufficiently rich formal system cannot prove its own consistency from within.
Heisenberg's uncertainty principle: one cannot simultaneously fix, with arbitrary
precision, complementary quantities such as position and momentum, and observation
itself disturbs the observed. The second law of thermodynamics: closed systems tend
toward increasing entropy. His synthesis: any mental model, treated as a closed
system, will drift out of correspondence with a changing reality (entropy rises),
cannot validate itself from inside (Godel), and cannot be perfected by ever-finer
observation (Heisenberg). Therefore survival demands a continuous, open cycle of
destroying and rebuilding one's orientation - which is precisely what the OODA loop
institutionalizes.


6. COMMON MISINTERPRETATIONS

The dominant popular and managerial version of OODA is wrong in three specific ways.
First, it is drawn as a simple, one-directional four-step cycle, erasing the
multiple feedback loops that make the system adaptive. Second, and most seriously,
it omits the Implicit Guidance and Control links - the two arrows from Orient that
are the fast, intuitive path of the expert and, arguably, the whole point of the
model; in the pop version every decision must pass through an explicit Decide box,
exactly the slow behavior Boyd was trying to transcend. Third, it treats the four
stages as co-equal, losing the primacy of Orientation as the schwerpunkt that
shapes all the others.


7. MODERN APPLICATIONS

Despite living only in briefings, the OODA loop has propagated widely: into United
States Marine Corps maneuver-warfare doctrine (which prizes tempo, decentralized
initiative, and shattering enemy cohesion over attrition); into business strategy
(getting inside a competitor's OODA loop through faster product cycles and superior
organizational orientation); into cybersecurity incident response (observe
telemetry, orient against threat intelligence, decide on containment, act, cycling
faster than an attacker); into litigation, law enforcement, and emergency response;
into sports and agile software; and, increasingly, into AI agents and autonomous
systems, where OODA is used as an architectural template (perceive, then model and
orient, then plan and decide, then act). Recent commentary warns that tool-using AI
agents each run nested, interleaving OODA loops, and that their inputs and tool
descriptions become injection vectors that corrupt the Orient step - a modern echo
of Boyd's insight that whoever controls orientation controls the outcome. The
through-line across every domain is Boyd's core thesis: victory belongs not to the
strongest or even the fastest per se, but to whoever can most rapidly and accurately
re-orient to a changing reality, and deny that same ability to the adversary.


8. HOW OODA MAPS ONTO MENTOVA'S HIERARCHICAL PLANNER

Mentova's co_hplan pack makes the OODA loop the explicit middle layer of every plan.
The correspondence is deliberate and close.

 • The middle-layer phases are Boyd's loop, with two of them named for the parts
   that the cartoon version drops. co_hplan's phases are see, observe, orient,
   decide, act, and re-observe-and-update-the-rules. See and observe together are
   Boyd's Observe fed by the unfolding grid; orient is Boyd's Orient; decide is his
   Decide; act is his Act; and re-observe-and-update-the-rules is the feedback from
   Act back to Observe that closes the loop and revises the model - Boyd's insistence
   that acting is a test whose result must be re-observed and folded back in.

 • Orientation is the schwerpunkt in Mentova too. The orient phase decomposes into
   applying the cross-game priors, identifying the game's archetype, and ranking the
   objects and hazards - which is Boyd's analysis-and-synthesis over previous
   experience (the twenty-five studied games), cultural tradition (the priors), and
   new information (the current grid). The quality of this phase governs everything
   downstream, exactly as Boyd held.

 • The implicit-guidance shortcut has a direct analog. Boyd's fast path runs from
   Orient to Act without an explicit Decide when a situation matches a known pattern.
   In Mentova, the object-targeted curiosity and the replay of a learned winning path
   are that shortcut: when orientation recognizes the situation, the player goes
   straight to touching the salient object or replaying the known win, rather than
   deliberating over the full policy. The explicit decide phase (causal-change
   ranking, the state-graph frontier, least-tried curiosity) is used when the
   situation is novel and no pattern fits.

 • Destroy and rebuild the model. Boyd's destruction-and-creation is Mentova's
   re-observe-and-update phase: the verify-before-act world model and the death
   memory are continually revised as the grid teaches new lessons, and an obsolete
   expectation is discarded when the observed delta contradicts it.

 • The whole plan is reified onto Causalontology's decomposition hierarchy, so the
   OODA loop is not a picture beside the reasoning but a hierarchy of causal
   relations the mind can inspect, and the glass-box why endpoint names which OODA
   phase produced each action. This is orientation made transparent - the opposite
   of the injection risk that corrupts an opaque agent's Orient step.

Holding this methodology as a skill in Mentova's Jacobian-Space workspace lets the
mind treat OODA not only as the shape of its plans but as a concept it can reason
about: which phase it is in, why orientation dominates, and when to take the fast
implicit path rather than the slow explicit one.
