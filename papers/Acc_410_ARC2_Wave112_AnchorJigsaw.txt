TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 446ef5d2 presents two training pairs (grids 12x20 and 10x10
on background 8) and one test case (a 26x26 grid on background
4). Every input scatters ragged colored FRAGMENTS across the
grid - four fragments in each training input, ten in the test
input, two of them non-rectangular - plus one three-cell
L-TROMINO MARKER drawn in a color that appears nowhere else
(color 4 in training, color 2 in the test).

The marker is a corner bracket. Its three cells fill a two-by-two
bounding box except for one missing cell: the CUPPED CELL P. The
diagonal step from the marker's corner cell K to P points into
the picture: it names which corner of the finished picture sits
exactly on P (bottom-right in train 1, top-left in train 2,
bottom-left in the test).

The fragments are jigsaw pieces. They tile, by translation alone
- no rotations, no reflections - a single rectangle whose area
equals their total cell count (7x11 = 77 in train 1, 6x6 = 36 in
train 2, 22x10 = 220 in the test). The piece containing P is the
ANCHOR PIECE: it keeps its absolute grid position, so the whole
picture crystallizes around a piece that never moves. The
rectangle's outer ring is uniformly the border color - which is
simply the fragment color found at P. The output erases the
marker and the scattered fragments and paints the assembled
rectangle with its named corner on P.

The discriminating observation of the wave is the COHERENCE LAW.
An exact-cover tiler armed only with the uniform-border
constraint finds TWO tilings of the 22x10 test rectangle - and
the wrong one first. In the false assembly the color-3 frame and
the color-7 frame are each torn into two separated clusters; in
the true assembly every non-border color forms exactly ONE
4-connected component - three clean nested rings of colors 3, 6,
and 7 stacked in a column of 1s. Demanding one component per
non-border color rejects the false picture and accepts the true
one, and the same law holds silently on both training pairs.

RULE: anchor_jigsaw

The glass-box rule is a five-stage procedure whose parameters are
all read off the input grid at solve time:

1. MARKER AND CUPPED CELL (aj_marker_/4)
   The background is the most common color. The marker is the
   color occupying exactly three cells in the whole grid, the
   three filling a two-by-two box minus one cell; that missing
   cell is P. The marker cell diagonal from P is the corner K,
   and the step from K to P names the picture corner that sits
   on P.

2. PIECES AND ANCHOR (arc2_sx_comps4_/2, aj_piece_/3)
   Every 4-connected component of non-background, non-marker
   cells is a piece, normalized to its bounding-box origin with
   its colors attached. The piece containing P is the anchor
   piece, and the border color B is the input color at P.

3. FACTORIZATION AND TILING (aj_assemble_/11, aj_tile_/6)
   The picture area A is the total piece cell count. For every
   height-width factorization of A that fits the tallest and the
   widest piece, the anchor piece is pinned at the P-corner and
   the remaining pieces are tiled by backtracking: the piece
   whose row-major first cell covers the first empty cell is
   placed next, translation only, with no overlaps, no
   out-of-bounds cells, and every cell landing on the outer ring
   carrying the border color B (aj_place_/8, aj_first_empty_/5).

4. COHERENCE FILTER (aj_coherent_/2)
   Among all complete assemblies, keep those in which every
   non-border color forms exactly one 4-connected component,
   and take the first.

5. RENDERING (aj_render_/7)
   The output is the background grid with the assembled
   rectangle painted so that its named corner sits exactly on P;
   the marker and all input fragments are gone.

VERIFICATION

The complete rule was written in plain English covering both
training pairs and the test case before any code, following the
proven wave method: all six grids were dumped to text and
analyzed programmatically. The programmatic dump immediately
corrected a hand-reading error - eyeballing the 26-column test
grid had misplaced the assembled rectangle by one row, which
falsely broke the anchor law; the machine diff restored it
exactly (P equals the picture corner in all three grids, and the
anchor piece stays in place in all three). A Python prototype
then confirmed the algorithm and caught the wave's one real
trap: with only the uniform-border constraint the tiler returned
two tilings of the test rectangle and preferred the false,
incoherent one. The coherence filter fixed it, after which the
prototype passed all three grids and the integrated Prolog rule
passed the single-task test and the full regression on its first
compile: induction returns anchor_jigsaw, the test output
matches exactly, and every previously solved task still passes.
Induction is pure verification: a cheap pre-filter (equal input
and output dimensions plus the presence of the unique-color
L-tromino marker) followed by exact transformation of every
training pair.

A lesson of independent interest: a three-cell L-tromino is the
minimal corner bracket, packing three commands into three cells
- WHERE the picture stands, WHICH corner touches the spot, and
WHICH piece never moves. And an exact-cover tiling that looks
airtight can still be ambiguous: the human jigsaw instinct that
a picture's frames must not fall apart into separated clusters
is a formal constraint, one connected component per color, and
it is exactly the constraint the puzzle demands.
