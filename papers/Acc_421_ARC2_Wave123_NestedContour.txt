TASK DESCRIPTION

The board is a two-color grid: a single line color drawn as big closed
loops, and a background color filling the areas between and inside them.
The loops nest - each loop encloses an area, that area may hold a
smaller loop, that smaller loop encloses a smaller area, and so on, down
to an innermost region. Several separate nests may share the grid.

In the output, the same nesting topology is redrawn at the smallest
possible scale. Every region becomes a one-cell-thick outline, the
innermost region collapses to a single dot, and where several nests sit
side by side they snap to a shared row or column so their innermost dots
line up. All the original distances, thicknesses, and coordinates are
discarded; only which region contains which, and how deep each sits,
survives.

THE RULE

The glass-box rule I call nested_contour works in four stages.

Stage 1 - regions. The background is the most common color. The grid is
split into connected regions: each background area as a four-connected
component, and each stretch of the line color as an eight-connected
component. This matches how a loop is drawn (a thin line whose cells
touch diagonally) against how an area spreads (filling orthogonally).

Stage 2 - containment tree. Each cell is tagged with its region, and
regions that share a four-neighbor edge are made adjacent. A
breadth-first search starts from every background region that touches
the grid border (the outer sea, depth zero) and walks inward, giving
each region a depth and a parent. The result is a tree: the outer sea
contains the outermost loops, each loop contains an area, each area
contains the next loop, and so on to the innermost region.

Stage 3 - render. Each top-level region is rendered recursively. A leaf
region (nothing inside it) becomes a single cell of its own color - the
innermost dot. An internal region renders each of its children, arranges
them, and then wraps the arranged block in a one-cell-thick frame of its
own color. Because loops and areas alternate down the tree, the wrapping
alternates line color and background, producing the nested outlines.

Stage 4 - arrange and pad. When a region holds several children, they
are placed by clustering their innermost-dot anchors into row bands and
column bands (two anchors join a band when they sit within three cells
of each other), then aligning each child so its anchor lands on the
shared band line, with the gaps filled by the parent's color. Finally,
if a lone innermost dot ends up touching a grid edge, that edge is
padded with one row or column of background so the dot keeps a margin.

THE DISCRIMINATING OBSERVATION

The heart of the rule is the separation of nesting topology from
absolute geometry. The output preserves only the containment tree - who
is inside whom, and how deep - and rebuilds the picture at minimal
scale. Every original size, wall thickness, and coordinate is thrown
away and regenerated from the tree alone. Sibling nests are then
re-registered to one another by their innermost dots, not by their old
positions, which is why the anchor-alignment step is essential: it is
the only thing that decides where two shrunk nests sit relative to each
other once their original coordinates are gone.
