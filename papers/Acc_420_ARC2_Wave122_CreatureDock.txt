TASK DESCRIPTION

The board is a colored frame of structural bands - whole rows or whole
columns painted a single color - wrapped around a plain background
field. Floating inside the field are two kinds of shapes:

  Creatures: touching clusters that mix cyan (color 8) body cells, a
  maroon (color 9) core, and a few colored tips (single accent cells of
  other colors sticking out of the body).

  Locks: isolated maroon-only patches, each shaped exactly like the
  maroon core of one of the creatures.

In the output, every creature is lifted off the field and re-docked
onto the lock whose maroon shape matches its core. The creature is
rotated and, if needed, mirrored so that it sits over the lock in the
orientation that best aims its colored tips toward the frame.

THE RULE

Step 1 - Find the structural bands. Repeatedly mark any not-yet-marked
  row whose remaining (unmarked) columns all carry a single color, and
  any not-yet-marked column whose remaining rows all carry a single
  color, until neither pass adds anything. This fixpoint peels the frame
  apart even when no outer row or column is uniform on its own: a solid
  top band can hide the uniformity of a side band until the band above
  it is removed.

Step 2 - Identify the field and the frame. The background is the most
  common color among the non-structural cells. Border cells are the
  structural cells whose color differs from the background; each records
  its position and color. Foreground cells are the non-structural cells
  that are not background.

Step 3 - Group the pieces. Split the foreground into 8-connected
  components. A component that contains any cyan cell is a creature; a
  component whose cells are all maroon is a lock.

Step 4 - Clear the field. Erase every creature and every lock to the
  background color.

Step 5 - Re-dock each creature. For each creature, try every unused lock
  across all eight isometries (four rotations combined with two mirror
  states). Rotate the creature's maroon core; when its normalized shape
  equals the lock's shape, rotate the whole creature the same way and
  slide it so the core lands exactly on the lock. Then score the
  orientation: each colored tip earns one point when it sits on the same
  side of the lock's center as the border band that shares its color -
  columns are compared for a vertical (left or right) band, rows for a
  horizontal (top or bottom) band. Keep the highest-scoring orientation,
  paint it onto the cleared field, and mark that lock as used.

THE DISCRIMINATING OBSERVATION

The tip-to-border scoring is the key. A creature's maroon core is often
symmetric enough to fit a lock in several rotations or mirror states, so
the core shape alone does not fix the orientation. The colored tips
break the tie: the correct docking is the one whose tips point toward
the border bands that share their colors, judged relative to the lock's
center. This turns a purely geometric fit into a directed one, and it is
what reproduces the held-out test grid pixel-exactly.
