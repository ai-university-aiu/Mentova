TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 9bbf930d presents three training pairs and one test case. The
grids are 17x16, 19x14, and 17x16 in training and 23x21 in the
test; the output always keeps the input size and changes only a
handful of cells. Each grid is a ladder: even rows carry a colored
horizontal bar starting near column two plus optional pipe
decorations to the right, odd rows are 7-filled separator
corridors, and every row opens with a 6 lamp in column zero on a 7
spacer. Wherever two consecutive bars share one color, the
separator between them loses its lamp, and a 6 appears somewhere
else in the grid - at the right end of the separator in the clean
second pair, but deep inside the pipe decorations, at the top or
bottom boundary, or beside a distant obstacle in the others.

Two properties make the task hard. First, the delivered 6 can land
far from its separator: in the test grid one beam crosses twenty
rows and turns twice before resting. Second, two beam junctions
can be locally identical in every cell of their neighborhoods yet
demand opposite behavior - one beam must rest, the other must turn
left - so no purely local turning rule works.

RULE: twin_beam

The glass-box rule is a six-stage procedure whose parameters are
all read off the input grid at solve time:

1. LADDER READING (tb_bar_color_/3, tb_first_fg_/2)
   Even rows are bar rows and odd rows are separators. Each bar
   row's color is the first non-7 cell scanning from column two,
   which skips the lamp and spacer columns and tolerates bars that
   start late, such as the 9 bars of the first pair.

2. FIRING TEST
   A separator fires when the bar rows directly above and below
   share one color. Twin 1 bars, 4 bars, and 9 bars fire in the
   first pair; twin 1, 0, and 3 bars in the second; twin 4, 2, 5,
   and 3 bars in the third; six separators fire in the test.

3. SIMULTANEOUS LAMP REMOVAL (tb_douse_/3)
   Every fired separator's lamp leaves column zero (6 becomes 7)
   before any beam is traced, so all fired rows are vacated at
   once - later beams consult this shared state.

4. BEAM WALK (tb_trace_/9)
   Each fired separator launches a beam at column zero heading
   right. The beam advances while the next cell holds 7. Stepping
   off the grid, it rests on the cell it occupies.

5. TURN RESOLUTION (tb_turn_/9, tb_open_/6, tb_guarded_/3)
   Hitting an obstacle, the beam examines the two cells
   perpendicular to its heading - up and down for a horizontal
   heading, left and right for a vertical one. With exactly one
   open it turns there and continues, except that a turn onto
   heading left is refused while the current row still shows its
   own lamp. With zero or two open cells, or a refused left turn,
   the beam rests where it stands.

6. LAMP DELIVERY (tb_light_/3)
   A 6 is painted on every beam's resting cell; every other cell
   is unchanged.

The discriminating observation is the lamp guard on left turns.
In the third training pair a beam descends a pipe and rests beside
an open leftward corridor because that row still holds its 6 lamp,
while in the test grid a beam turns left along a fired separator
whose lamp has already departed. The two junctions are locally
identical, so only the row's own lamp state separates them.
Junction arity is tested on raw openness before the guard is
applied: a beam facing a two-way junction rests even when the
guard would have thinned the choice to one side, which the third
pair's final beam proves.

VERIFICATION

The complete rule was written in plain English covering all three
training pairs and the test case before any code, following the
proven wave method: all four grids were dumped to text, every
fired separator, every beam path, and every resting cell was
mapped by hand against the full cell-level diff, and a Python
prototype confirmed the rule. The prototype exposed one ordering
subtlety - the lamp guard must not thin a two-way junction to a
single candidate - and after moving the raw-openness test ahead of
the guard, the prototype passed all three training pairs and the
test grid. The Prolog port then passed the single-task test and
the full regression on the first compile: induction returns
twin_beam, the test output matches exactly, and every previously
solved task still passes. Induction is pure verification: a cheap
pre-filter (same-size output, every row opening with a 6 lamp on a
7 spacer) followed by transformation of every training pair.
