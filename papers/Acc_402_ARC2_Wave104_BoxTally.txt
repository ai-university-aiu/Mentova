TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 8f215267 presents three training pairs and one test case. All
grids are 23 columns wide and 20 to 27 rows tall, and the output
always keeps the input size. Each input shows, along the left side,
a stack of hollow rectangular boxes - one-cell-thick frames five
rows tall and eleven columns wide, each in its own color, each with
a pure background interior. The rest of the grid is sprinkled with
small scattered shapes of assorted colors: dominoes, two-by-two
squares, two-by-three blocks, plus signs, and diamonds.

In the output the scatter is gone. Every box frame stands exactly
where it stood, its interior clean, except for a short row of dots
of the box's own color printed on the middle interior row -
right-aligned starting two columns left of the right frame edge and
spaced two cells apart. The number of dots is the answer to a
counting question: how many separate scatter pieces of this box's
color lie elsewhere in the grid? A box whose color has no scatter
prints no dots at all, and scatter colors owning no box simply
vanish with everything else.

The heart of the task is what exactly is being counted. In the
first training pair the color-8 scatter totals ten cells spread
over four pieces of four different shapes - a horizontal domino,
a two-by-two square, a vertical domino, and another horizontal
domino - and the color-8 box prints exactly four dots. Cells,
areas, and shapes all disagree; only the count of separate
4-connected components fits every box in every pair. Distractor
colors reinforce the reading: color 3 scatter in the first
training pair has no color-3 box and is silently erased, as are
colors 5 and 9 in the test grid.

RULE: box_tally

The glass-box rule is a four-stage procedure whose parameters are
all read off the input grid at solve time:

1. SCENE READING (td_background_/2, bt_fg_cells_/3, bt_colors_/2)
   The most frequent color is the background. Every non-background
   cell is collected with its position and color, and the distinct
   foreground colors are listed.

2. COMPONENT CLASSIFICATION (bt_color_pts_/3, bt_comps_/2,
   bt_grow_/4, bt_hollow_box_/1, bt_bbox_/2, bt_border_/2)
   Each color's cells are grouped into 4-connected components by
   flood fill. A component whose cells are exactly the border of
   its own bounding box, spanning at least three rows and three
   columns, is a hollow box; every other component is a scatter
   shape.

3. TALLYING (bt_color_boxes_/3)
   For each color, the scatter components are counted and every
   box of that color receives an entry carrying its bounding box
   and the scatter tally.

4. RENDERING (bt_blank_/4, bt_draw_entry_/3, bt_draw_frame_/4,
   bt_draw_dots_/5)
   A blank background canvas of the input size is created, every
   box frame is redrawn in place, and each box prints one dot of
   its color per tallied scatter component on the middle interior
   row, at columns right-edge minus two, minus four, minus six,
   and so on.

The discriminating observation is that the tally counts components
rather than cells. The four color-8 pieces of the first training
pair carry two, four, two, and two cells - ten in total - yet the
box prints four dots; the single six-cell color-2 block prints one
dot; and the zero color-2 pieces of the third training pair leave
that box empty. Only the component count fits all twelve boxes
across the four grids.

VERIFICATION

The complete rule was written in plain English covering all three
training pairs and the test case before any code, following the
proven wave method: all eight grids were dumped to text, every
scatter component of every color was counted by hand, and every
dot position was checked against the right-aligned two-spacing
formula in every box, including the empty color-2 box of the third
training pair. A standalone prototype confirmed the reading on all
four pairs before implementation began. The integrated Prolog rule
passed the single-task test and the full regression on its first
compile: induction returns box_tally, the test output matches
exactly, and every previously solved task still passes. Induction
is pure verification: a cheap pre-filter (same-size output, at
least two foreground colors, at least two hollow boxes with pure
background interiors, all box colors distinct) followed by
transformation of every training pair.
