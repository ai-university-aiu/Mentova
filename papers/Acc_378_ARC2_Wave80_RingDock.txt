TASK DESCRIPTION

Task b5ca7ac4 presents three training pairs and one test case. The
training grids are 22x22 and the test grid is 22x25. Each grid
scatters six to eight five-by-five ring objects over a solid
background - every ring is a one-cell uniform border of color 8 or
color 2 around a solid three-by-three uniform interior of some other
color. In the output every 8-bordered ring has slid horizontally into
a parking lane against the left edge and every 2-bordered ring
against the right edge, each ring keeping its rows exactly.
Same-side rings whose rows overlap pack side by side against the
ring already parked nearer the edge, and rings starting on the wrong
side of the grid cross the whole board to reach their lane.

RULE: ring_dock(L, R)

The glass-box rule is a four-stage docking procedure whose two
learned parameters are the left-going and right-going border colors:

1. RING DETECTION (rd_ring_/3, rd_rings_/3)
   The background is the most frequent color. Every five-by-five ring
   is detected by scanning all top-left corners for a sixteen-cell
   uniform border of a non-background color around a nine-cell
   uniform interior of a different non-background color. A
   total-coverage guard demands that the grid's non-background cell
   count equal exactly twenty-five times the ring count, so the
   detector can never miss content or fire on a false positive.

2. DIRECTION ASSIGNMENT (induce clause)
   The rings split by border color into left movers and right movers.
   The assignment of the two border colors to the two directions is
   the induced rule parameter pair ring_dock(L, R), found by trying
   both assignments and keeping the one under which every training
   pair transforms correctly.

3. PER-SIDE PACKING (rd_place_left_/4, rd_place_right_/5)
   Each side gets its own fresh background canvas. Left movers sort
   by ascending column - edge-nearest first, ties broken by row - and
   each in turn slides leftward one column at a time until the grid
   edge or an already-parked same-side ring blocks it, then stamps
   its five-by-five block onto the canvas. Right movers do the mirror
   image toward the right edge.

4. CANVAS MERGE (rd_merge_/4)
   The two side canvases merge cell by cell into the output,
   preferring non-background cells. Opposite-side rings never collide
   during sliding because they park on separate canvases - which is
   exactly why input rings on opposite sides of the grid can trade
   places freely.

THE KEY INSIGHT: COLLISION SCOPED TO DESTINATION

The first prototype slid every ring over a single shared canvas, and
it failed two of the three training pairs: a right mover was blocked
by a left mover sitting in its path, even though the training outputs
show the two groups passing through each other - an 8-ring starting
at the right edge and a 2-ring starting at the left edge trade
places. The load-bearing insight is that collision must be scoped to
objects with the same destination: a physical single-board simulation
is the wrong model even though it feels more realistic. Splitting the
simulation into one canvas per side and merging afterward fixed all
three training pairs at once, and the test case passed on the same
run. Ordering movers by distance to their target edge makes stepwise
sliding equivalent to final-position packing, keeping the simulation
loop trivially glass-box.

INDUCTION PRE-FILTER

The first training pair must preserve the grid dimensions, the first
input must contain at least one detected ring, and the rings must
carry exactly two distinct border colors. These guards are cheap and
exclude every other task family before the full forall verification
runs. The induce clause dispatches early, directly after
master_fragment, ahead of the generic named-rule search.

KEY PREDICATES

  rd_ring_/3           - detect one five-by-five ring object
  rd_rings_/3          - collect every ring object in the grid
  rd_canvas_/4         - build an all-background canvas
  rd_free_/4           - a five-by-five region is empty on the canvas
  rd_slide_left_/5     - slide a ring leftward until blocked
  rd_slide_right_/6    - slide a ring rightward until blocked
  rd_draw_/5           - stamp a five-by-five block onto the canvas
  rd_place_left_/4     - park all left movers in edge-nearest order
  rd_place_right_/5    - park all right movers in edge-nearest order
  rd_merge_/4          - overlay the two side canvases
  arc2_transform(ring_dock(L, R), +Grid, -Out)
  arc2_induce_rule(+Pairs, ring_dock(L, R))

VERIFICATION

All three training pairs and the test case pass in SWI-Prolog. One
bug was found and fixed during prototyping (the shared-canvas
collision model described above). A full regression of all 76 locally
verified tasks (75 prior plus b5ca7ac4) still induces and passes.
Pure induction from training examples. No pretraining on the
ARC-AGI-2 corpus. Every rule is named and glass-box.
