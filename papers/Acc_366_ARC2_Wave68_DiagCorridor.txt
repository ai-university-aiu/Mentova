TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 7666fa5d presents 16x16 grids with background color 8 and a single
bank color per grid (4, 3, or 9). The bank cells form slanted anti-diagonal
chains - runs of cells stepping (row+1, column-1) - that act as the two
banks of a winding corridor. Every background cell lying between a pair of
opposing banks must be flooded with color 2 (red), filling the corridor;
the banks themselves and all unbracketed background stay untouched.

RULE: diag_corridor

The glass-box named rule is a four-stage coordinate-transform bracketing
procedure with no learned parameters:

1. BACKGROUND AND BANKS (dc_bg_/2, dc_banks_/3)
   The background color is the most frequent cell value; every other cell
   is a bank cell.

2. CHAIN EXTRACTION (dc_chains_/2, dc_grow_/5)
   Bank cells are grouped into chains: connected components under the
   anti-diagonal step (r+1, c-1). Every cell of a chain shares the same
   line sum s = r + c, and the chain spans an interval [dmin, dmax] in the
   cross coordinate d = r - c (dc_chain_info_/4).

3. CORRIDOR BRACKETING (dc_fill_cell_/5)
   For every unordered pair of chains with distinct line sums s1 < s2, a
   background cell belongs to their corridor when its line sum satisfies
   s1 < r + c < s2 and its cross coordinate d = r - c lies inside the
   intersection [max(dmin1, dmin2), min(dmax1, dmax2)] of the two chains'
   cross intervals.

4. FLOOD (dc_paint_/4)
   The union of all corridor regions over all chain pairs is painted with
   color 2; bank cells and unbracketed background cells are preserved.

THE KEY INSIGHT: A CHANGE OF COORDINATES

In ordinary (row, column) space the corridors wind diagonally and the fill
looks irregular: a background cell directly adjacent to a bank may stay
empty while a cell farther away is filled. In the rotated coordinate
system (s, d) with s = r + c and d = r - c, each chain becomes an
axis-aligned segment (constant s, an interval in d), and the corridor
between two banks becomes a plain rectangle: strictly between in s,
interval intersection in d. The winding corridor is straight when viewed
along the diagonals.

Two subtleties matter. First, ALL pairs of chains contribute corridors,
not merely adjacent pairs: in training pair 2, cells such as (8,13) are
bracketed only by the outermost pair of chains, whose corridor covers
regions no adjacent pair reaches. Second, two distinct chains can share
the same line sum - in training pair 1 two separate chains both sit on
s = 12 at opposite ends of the grid - so chains must be separated by
anti-diagonal connectivity; grouping bank cells by line sum alone would
merge them and create phantom corridors.

INDUCTION PRE-FILTER

The first training pair must have equal input and output row counts, the
input must contain exactly two colors (background plus one bank color),
and the output palette must equal the input palette plus fill color 2;
all training pairs are then verified with forall before the rule is
selected.

KEY PREDICATES

  dc_bg_/2          - most frequent cell value as background color
  dc_banks_/3       - sorted coordinates of all non-background cells
  dc_chains_/2      - anti-diagonal connected components of bank cells
  dc_grow_/5        - extend a chain by the next (r+1, c-1) neighbor
  dc_chain_info_/4  - line sum and cross-coordinate range of a chain
  dc_fill_cell_/5   - background cell bracketed by a pair of chains
  dc_paint_/4       - repaint bracketed cells with color 2
  arc2_transform(diag_corridor, +Grid, -Out)
  arc2_induce_rule(+Pairs, diag_corridor)
