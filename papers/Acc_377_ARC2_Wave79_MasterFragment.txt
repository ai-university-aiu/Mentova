TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 269e22fb presents five training pairs and one test case. The
inputs are 8x10, 16x8, 10x10, 13x12, and 8x10 grids, and the test
input is 10x10; every output is 20x20. Each grid uses exactly two
colors - four pairs use colors 7 and 8, one pair uses colors 0 and 3.
Every training output is one shared canonical two-color 20x20 master
pattern, reproduced up to one of the eight dihedral isometries and
one of the two possible two-color bijections, and every input is a
contiguous rectangular fragment cut directly from its own output.
The answer is the full master pattern rendered in the fragment's
orientation and colors.

RULE: master_fragment(Master)

The glass-box rule is a four-stage fragment-location procedure whose
single learned parameter is the canonical master grid itself:

1. MASTER CAPTURE (induce clause)
   Induction stores the first training output verbatim as the
   canonical master pattern. Cheap guards come first: the output must
   be at least as tall and as wide as the input and strictly larger
   overall, the master must use exactly two colors, and every
   training output must share the master dimensions exactly.

2. CANDIDATE ENUMERATION (mf_iso_/2, mf_recolor_/3)
   The transform reads the two colors of the fragment and the two
   colors of the master, then enumerates the eight dihedral
   isometries of the master - identity, three rotations, two axis
   mirrors, and two diagonal mirrors, built from the registry
   transforms rotate_90_cw, rotate_180, rotate_90_ccw, reverse_rows,
   vertical_flip, and transpose - crossed with the two possible
   two-color bijections onto the fragment palette: sixteen full-size
   candidates in all.

3. FRAGMENT CONTAINMENT (mf_subgrid_/2)
   A candidate survives only when the input fragment appears inside
   it as an exact contiguous subgrid, checked by sliding the fragment
   over every feasible row and column offset and demanding cell-for-
   cell equality.

4. UNIQUENESS (sort/2 to a singleton)
   The surviving candidates are deduplicated and exactly one
   full-size grid must remain. That unique candidate is the answer,
   so an ambiguous fragment can never produce a guessed output - the
   transform simply fails instead.

THE KEY INSIGHT: A GRID-VALUED RULE PARAMETER

This is the first wave whose induced rule carries a whole learned
grid as a rule argument - master_fragment(Master) - rather than
colors or scalar parameters. The parameterized-rule machinery
introduced for recolor_plus(A, B) extends cleanly to grid-valued
parameters: the induce clause binds the master from the first
training output, and the transform treats it as data. The uniqueness
demand doubles as both the correctness proof and the induction
verifier - each of the five training pairs admits exactly one
candidate among sixteen, so the rule is self-checking, and the same
check answered the test case on the first run. The human-steps
description framed the task as recalling a memorized master pattern;
the clean formulation - master equals first output, fragment located
by isometry-times-bijection search - needs no memorization beyond
the training pairs themselves and stays fully glass-box.

INDUCTION PRE-FILTER

The first training pair must have an output at least as tall and as
wide as its input and strictly larger overall, the output must use
exactly two colors, and all training outputs must share the same
dimensions. These guards are near constant-time and exclude every
other task family before the full forall verification runs. The
induce clause dispatches early, directly after gadget_assembly,
ahead of the generic named-rule search.

KEY PREDICATES

  mf_colors_/2         - sorted list of the distinct cell colors

  mf_recolor_row_/3    - repaint one row through the bijection
  mf_recolor_cell_/3   - translate one color through the bijection
