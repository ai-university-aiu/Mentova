The glass-box rule I call creature_dock:

Step 1: find the structural bands with a fixpoint that peels uniform rows and columns, one after another, even when no outer line is uniform on its own - a solid top band can hide the uniformity of a side band until it is removed.

Step 2: the background is the most common non-structural color; the frame's colored cells become border bands, each tagged with its color.

Step 3: split the foreground into 8-connected pieces - a piece with any cyan cell is a creature, an all-maroon piece is a lock.

Step 4: clear every creature and lock off the field.

Step 5: re-dock each creature onto the lock whose maroon shape matches its core, trying all eight orientations (four rotations by two mirrors) and keeping the one whose colored tips best point toward the border band that shares their color, measured from the lock's center.

The discriminating detail is the tip-to-border scoring. A maroon core is often symmetric enough to fit a lock several ways, so shape alone leaves the orientation ambiguous. The colored tips resolve it: the right docking aims each tip toward its own color's border band.
