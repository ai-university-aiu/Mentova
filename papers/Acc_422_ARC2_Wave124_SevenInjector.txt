TASK DESCRIPTION

The board scatters small blue markers shaped like the letter T: a
three-cell straight bar plus a single stalk cell attached at the middle
of the bar, so the stalk points away from the bar in one of four
directions. Some Ts are drawn entirely in blue; others keep their two
bar ends and their stalk blue but hold a different color in the center
of the bar. Separately, the board may carry one isolated dark-red glyph
shaped like a seven, or its mirror image.

In the output, each T marks a nearby region for recoloring. The region
just in front of the stalk tip is repainted: an injecting T (one whose
center holds a non-blue color) paints that center color, while a plain
all-blue T paints the color of the field that surrounds it. Every T is
then erased, blending back into its background. Finally, if a dark-red
seven glyph is present, the whole board is given a single quarter-turn:
a seven turns it counter-clockwise, and a mirror seven turns it
clockwise.

THE RULE

The glass-box rule I call seven_injector works in seven stages.

Stage 1 - candidates. For every cell and every one of four orientations
(stalk up, down, left, or right), the rule tests for a T: the two bar
ends and the stalk must be blue, and the cell opposite the stalk must
not be blue. For each valid T it derives a home color (the dominant
color surrounding the four T cells, ignoring blue) and an injection
color (the center color for an injecting T, or the surrounding color for
a plain blue T).

Stage 2 - selection. Plain all-blue Ts are considered first, then
injecting Ts. Each T is accepted only if none of its four cells has
already been claimed by an earlier T, giving a disjoint set of injectors.

Stage 3 - seven search. The board is scanned for the first isolated
dark-red seven or mirror-seven glyph - five dark-red cells in the seven
shape with no other dark-red cell orthogonally adjacent.

Stage 4 - erase. Every selected T is overwritten with its home color on
a working copy, and any seven glyph is overwritten with the color that
surrounds it.

Stage 5 - heal. Any blue cell left over after erasing is repaired by a
breadth-first search outward to its nearest non-blue neighbor, copying
that neighbor's color. Cells are healed in row-major order, so a repair
can build on repairs already made above and to the left.

Stage 6 - inject. For each T, the region directly in front of the stalk
tip is flood-filled on the cleaned grid and painted with that T's
injection color.

Stage 7 - rotate. If a seven glyph was found, the whole board is rotated
a quarter turn: counter-clockwise for a left seven, clockwise for a
mirror seven.

THE DISCRIMINATING OBSERVATION

The heart of the rule is the separation of local injection from the
global rotation. The T injectors and the leftover-blue healing act
cell-locally - each T touches only the small region its stalk points at,
and each stray blue cell copies only its nearest neighbor. The seven
glyph, by contrast, is a single board-wide switch that reorients the
entire result. A solver that tried to fold the rotation into the local
painting, or that ignored the glyph, would reproduce the training pairs
that happen to share one orientation yet fail the moment the held-out
test flips. Reading the seven as a pure orientation instruction, applied
once at the very end, is what makes one rule serve every pair.

A second subtlety is the tie-breaking in the surround-color count. When
two colors border a T equally, the reference solver picks the one seen
first; the Prolog port reproduces this by counting neighbor colors in
encounter order and keeping the earliest on a tie, so the home and
injection colors match the reference exactly.
