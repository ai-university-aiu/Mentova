TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 5545f144 presents three training pairs and one test case. The
training inputs are 10x26, 8x27, and 12x25 grids split into two,
four, and two equal panels by full-height columns of a single
non-background color; the test input is a single 15x15 panel with
no separators at all. The output always keeps one panel's size.

Each panel shows cells of one foreground color: a multi-cell
shape - the crawler - plus a sprinkling of single cells - the
dots. The panels are not layers to be combined. They are TIME:
successive snapshots of one crawl. The dots are not noise but a
route. Consecutive stops always share a row or a column, the
crawler's protruding nose cell rides exactly on top of each
visited dot, and dots already visited vanish from later panels.
In the second training pair the four-cell crawler walks up its
own column, turns right along the top, and descends the far
column, its body swinging around the nose at every turn.

The output shows only the final frame: a clean background panel
with the crawler docked nose-first on the last dot of the route,
its body rotated by the quarter turn that points the nose along
the direction of the final leg.

RULE: dot_crawler

The glass-box rule is a five-stage procedure whose parameters are
all read off the input grid at solve time:

1. PANEL SPLITTING (dc_panels_/3, dc_sep_col_/3, dc_ranges_/4,
   dc_slice_/4)
   The most frequent color is the background. Any full-height
   column of one uniform non-background color is a separator; the
   grid is split at the separators and the leftmost panel carries
   the scene to solve, fixing the output size. A grid without
   separators is its own single panel.

2. SCENE READING (bt_fg_cells_/3, dc_comps8_/2, dc_grow8_/4,
   dc_by_size_/2)
   The panel's foreground cells - all of one color - are grouped
   into 8-connected components. The largest component is the
   crawler shape; every single-cell component is a dot.

3. NOSE SELECTION (dc_nose_cand_/5, dc_extreme_cell_/4,
   dc_first_dot_/4, dc_chain_/4, dc_nearest_/2)
   A nose candidate is a shape cell that sits alone on the top,
   bottom, left, or right edge of the shape's bounding box,
   pointing outward. Each candidate fires a ray along its
   direction to the nearest dot in line, then greedily hops to
   the nearest remaining dot sharing a row or column. Only a
   candidate whose chain consumes every dot is valid.

4. DISAMBIGUATION (dc_pick_nose_/7, dc_panel_dots_/3)
   When two candidates both consume every dot - the same chain
   read forward and backward - the second panel breaks the tie:
   the true first stop is already consumed or covered by the
   shape there, while the false first stop still sits free as a
   dot.

5. DOCKING (dc_arrival_/3, dc_dir_/3, dc_turns_/3, dc_cw_/2,
   dc_rot_offs_/3, bt_blank_/4)
   The shape is expressed as offsets from the nose, the offsets
   are rotated by the clockwise quarter turns that carry the nose
   heading onto the direction of the last chain leg, and the
   rotated shape is painted on a blank background panel with the
   nose exactly on the final dot.

The discriminating observation is the full-consumption nose test.
The second training grid presents two lone edge cells, and both
thread complete chains through all four dots - exact reverses of
each other. Only the snapshot panels reveal which end of the route
the crawler actually walks: the second panel already shows the
crawler covering the true first stop.

VERIFICATION

The complete rule was written in plain English covering all three
training pairs and the test case before any code, following the
proven wave method: all eight grids plus the test pair were dumped
to text, every dot of every panel was accounted for by hand, every
chain was walked stop by stop, and every output cell was checked
against the rotated docking formula. A standalone Python prototype
confirmed the reading on all four grids before implementation
began. The integrated Prolog rule passed the single-task test and
the full regression on its first compile: induction returns
dot_crawler, the test output matches exactly, and every previously
solved task still passes. Induction is pure verification: a cheap
pre-filter (same-height output, output width equal to the leftmost
panel width, a single foreground color, one crawler of at least
three cells, all other components single dots) followed by
transformation of every training pair.
