TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 4e34c42c presents two training pairs and one test case. The
inputs are 23x25, 30x20, and 26x20 scenes; the outputs are 5x12,
5x22, and 14x19. Each scene scatters several multi-color pieces on
a uniform background. The pieces carry duplicated connector stubs:
a same-color cell cluster drawn identically on two different
pieces - a 9,1,4,1,9 column, a 5-3 checkerboard corner, a
three-cell bar of 8s, or a lone pair of 2 marker cells. The output
is the single assembly obtained by welding the pieces together so
that each duplicated stub is superimposed on its twin exactly.

Two properties make the task hard. First, the assembly geometry
changes shape: both training outputs are linear horizontal chains
five rows tall, while the test output is a 14x19 two-dimensional
tree of six pieces welded above, below, left, and right - so the
output size and layout must follow the welds, not the training
dimensions. Second, each scene also carries a small key piece that
exactly duplicates one end of a larger piece; it welds on top of
that end and adds nothing to the union bounding box.

RULE: seam_weld

The glass-box rule is a five-stage procedure whose parameters are
all read off the input grid at solve time:

1. PIECE EXTRACTION (sw_pieces_/3, sw_records_/5)
   The most common color is the background. The non-background
   cells are grouped into side-connected multi-color pieces, each
   carried as its full bounding-box subgrid.

2. STUB INVENTORY (sw_parts_/5, sw_flood_/5)
   Every piece is split into whole same-color components under
   eight-way adjacency. These components are the candidate
   connector stubs.

3. CANVAS SEED (sw_write_/5)
   The piece holding the most non-background cells is written onto
   an empty canvas at the origin - its entire bounding box,
   background cells included.

4. WELDING LOOP (sw_assemble_/4, sw_placement_/12, sw_stub_/5)
   A placement of a piece at an offset is valid when every
   bounding-box cell landing on already-written canvas content
   agrees exactly - background included - and at least one whole
   same-color component of the piece lands entirely on written
   cells and coincides exactly with one whole same-color component
   of the canvas. Among all valid placements of all unplaced
   pieces, the earliest placement with the strictly highest count
   of agreeing non-background cells wins, the piece is written
   onto the canvas, and the loop repeats until every piece is
   placed.

5. RENDER (sw_render_/3)
   The union bounding box of the written canvas is emitted, with
   the background filling the unwritten cells.

The discriminating condition is component totality, not overlap
size. A Python prototype with plain greedy overlap welding - exact
agreement plus maximum overlap count - failed both the second
training pair and the test: it welded a piece's large 1-frame onto
another piece's 1-ring through three accidentally agreeing cells,
and glued a piece's 4-body onto another piece's 4-cap. The correct
weld always superimposes a complete same-color component of one
piece onto a complete same-color component of the other - the
duplicated stub both pieces carry - while spurious welds only ever
cover components partially. And because the smallest legitimate
weld in the task joins just two marker cells, no overlap-size
threshold can replace the totality condition.
