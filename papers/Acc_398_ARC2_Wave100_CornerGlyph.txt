TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 4c416de3 presents three training pairs and one test case. The
grids range from 19x22 to 23x23; the output always keeps the input
size. Each input holds boxes outlined in the zero border color on a
solid background - background 1, 8, 2, and 3 across the four grids -
some clipped by the grid edge. One box corner already carries a full
multi-cell decoration, the template, in its own color, and the
template shape differs in every grid: a two-by-two outward block
plus two inward diagonal cells in the first pair, a six-cell zigzag
band in the second, a four-cell corner cross in the third, and a
six-cell hook in the test. At other corners a single colored cell -
a glyph - sits at one specific position of the shape: its most
inward cell on the corner's own diagonal. The output completes
every glyph into the full template decoration, painted in the
glyph's color and mirrored into that corner's orientation, while
unmarked corners stay bare.

Two properties make the task hard. First, the shape must be learned
fresh from each grid's single decorated corner and re-oriented for
all four corner types, so the shape needs a corner-relative frame
rather than absolute offsets. Second, the decorations overwrite the
very borders that locate the corners: the template paints over the
border cells beside its own corner, and on a five-cell box side it
even sits within arm reach of the opposite corner, so a rigid
corner detector loses exactly the corners that must be stamped.

RULE: corner_glyph

The glass-box rule is a five-stage procedure whose parameters are
all read off the input grid at solve time:

1. BACKGROUND READING (arc2_bg_color_/2)
   The background is the most frequent color; it must differ from
   the zero border color.

2. CORNER DETECTION (cg_corner_/6, cg_arm_/6, cg_not_zero_/3)
   A box corner is a cell that is never background, paired with an
   outward orientation frame (u, v): two border arms run inward
   from it, horizontally and vertically, whose first four in-bounds
   cells are never background and keep at least two zero border
   cells each - decoration cells may overwrite border cells
   anywhere on the arm. The outward neighbors carry no border, and
   the inward diagonal cell is interior, never a border zero.

3. TEMPLATE LEARNING (cg_template_/4)
   The template corner is a detected corner with at least three
   cells of one color within Chebyshev distance two; glyph corners
   hold exactly one. Those cells become the shape, each offset
   stored in frame coordinates (p = dr times u, q = dc times v),
   so one canonical shape serves all four corner orientations.

4. MARKER POSITION (cg_marker_/2)
   The marker position is the shape's most inward offset on its
   own diagonal - p equals q, p negative, p minimal. This is where
   a glyph announces that its corner wants the decoration.

5. STAMPING (cg_stamp_/4, hb_paint_/4)
   Every detected corner whose marker cell holds a non-background,
   non-zero color receives the complete template shape stamped in
   that color, each offset transformed through the corner's own
   frame, with out-of-bounds stamp cells clipped away. Template
   corners repaint themselves unchanged, so the stamp is
   idempotent.

The discriminating observation is the tolerant arm test. The third
training pair's five-by-five box carries its template at one corner
and glyphs at two others; the template's cells lie on both border
arms of the adjacent corners at distances up to four, so an arm
test demanding exact zeros beyond distance two rejects those
corners and loses a required stamp. Forbidding only background
cells on the arm while demanding two surviving zeros admits every
true corner and, verified against all four grids, not one false
candidate: every impostor still fails on the zero count or on the
perpendicular arm.
