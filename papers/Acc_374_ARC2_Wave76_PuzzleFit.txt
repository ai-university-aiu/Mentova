TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 7b3084d4 presents 20x20 grids scattering four puzzle pieces over
the background. Each piece is a mostly single-color connected shape
with bumps and notches, and exactly one piece carries a single anchor
stud of color 5 in place of one of its own cells. The total number of
piece cells is always a perfect square - 36, 100, 16 across the three
training grids and 196 in the test grid. The output is the N-by-N
square assembled from the four pieces: every piece is rotated by some
number of quarter turns, the rotated pieces interlock with no gaps and
no overlaps, and the color-5 anchor stud lands exactly on the square's
top-left corner.

RULE: puzzle_fit

The glass-box named rule is a five-stage assembly procedure with no
learned parameters:

1. PIECE EXTRACTION (pf_cells_/3, pf_comps_/2)
   The background is the most common cell value. All non-background
   cells are grouped into 8-connected components over mixed colors -
   8-connected because some piece cells touch only diagonally, and
   mixed-color because the anchor stud's color 5 differs from its
   piece's body color.

2. NORMALIZATION (pf_norm_/2, pf_count_/3)
   Each piece is translated so its bounding box starts at row 0,
   column 0, and the piece cell counts are summed. The total must be
   a perfect square N * N, which fixes the output side length N.

3. ROTATION VARIANTS (pf_variants_/2, pf_map_/7)
   Each piece generates exactly four variants: identity and the three
   quarter-turn rotations. Reflections are deliberately excluded.

4. EXACT-COVER SEARCH (pf_solve_/3, pf_fill_/5)
   Backtracking fills the N-by-N board: at each step the scanline-first
   empty cell must be covered by the scanline-first cell of some
   rotated variant of a still-unplaced piece; the variant must fit the
   board and overlap only empty cells. The accepted solution is the
   tiling whose top-left corner carries the color-5 anchor stud.

5. RENDER (pf_render_/3)
   The placed cells are painted row by row into the output square,
   every cell keeping its input color, including the anchor stud.

THE KEY INSIGHT: ROTATIONS ONLY

Allowing the full dihedral group of eight variants per piece makes the
task ambiguous: under rotations plus reflections the three training
grids admit 3, 2, and 2 perfect tilings with the stud at the corner,
and the test grid admits 2, so any tie-break other than the right one
assembles a wrong square. Restricting the variants to the four
rotations collapses every grid - all three training grids and the test
grid - to exactly one perfect tiling, confirmed by exhaustive
enumeration. The pieces in this task are chiral enough that their
mirror images do not belong to the puzzle. A second, humbler lesson
came from the prototype: the counterclockwise rotation map accidentally
reused the input column variable as the output column, silently mapping
(R,C) to (W-1-C, C) instead of (W-1-C, R); the resulting zero-solution
search on a grid that had been solved by hand exposed the bug, flagged
all along by a singleton-variable warning.

INDUCTION PRE-FILTER

The first training pair's output must have strictly fewer rows and
strictly fewer columns than its input, be exactly square, carry color 5
in its top-left cell, and not carry color 5 in its bottom-left cell
(that shape belongs to glyph_swap). All training pairs are then
verified with forall before the rule is selected. The checks are
constant-time, keeping the pre-filter cheap for every other task in the
named-rule enumeration. The induce clause dispatches early, directly
after glyph_swap, ahead of the generic named-rule search.

KEY PREDICATES

  pf_cells_/3          - non-background cells as row-column-color
  pf_comps_/2          - 8-connected multi-color components
  pf_norm_/2           - piece shifted to bounding-box origin
  pf_variants_/2       - the four quarter-turn rotations, deduped
  pf_map_/7            - coordinate maps for id, r90, r180, r270
  pf_solve_/3          - N-by-N exact-cover tiling search
  pf_fill_/5           - scanline-anchored backtracking placement
  pf_render_/3         - placed cells painted as the output square
  arc2_bg_color_/2     - most common cell value as background (reused)
  arc2_transform(puzzle_fit, +Grid, -Out)
  arc2_induce_rule(+Pairs, puzzle_fit)

VERIFICATION

All three training pairs and the test case pass in SWI-Prolog, and
exhaustive enumeration confirms the rotation-only tiling is unique for
every grid. A regression spot-check confirms hole_census, glyph_swap,

layout_stack, frame_compass, shape_count, box_absorb, and

examples. No pretraining on the ARC-AGI-2 corpus. Every rule is named
and glass-box.
