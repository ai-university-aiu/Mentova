TASK DESCRIPTION

Task 9385bd28 presents four training pairs and one test case. In
each grid a two-column LEGEND sits at the bottom-left: a short
stack of rows in which the left column holds a marker color K and
the right column holds a fill color V, one K-to-V mapping per
row. Elsewhere in the grid, every marker color K paints two
three-cell L-TROMINOES - opposite corners of a rectangle. The
output draws that rectangle SOLID in the fill color V, keeping
the original marker cells, so the two corner brackets become a
filled box.

When several rectangles overlap, a stacking order decides which
fill is visible. In all four training pairs the rectangles NEST:
a small rectangle sits wholly inside a larger one, and the small
one shows on top. The original rule captured this with the
natural heuristic "smallest bounding-box area draws last," and it
passed every training pair.

The held-out test is a 19x13 grid on background 9 with a
five-row legend. Its rectangles do not nest - they CROSS. A
color-2 rectangle only two columns wide runs the full height of
the grid, a vertical stripe, and it passes straight through three
horizontal bars (colors 1, 5, and 3), none of which contains the
stripe or one another. At the twelve cells where the stripe meets
the bars, the correct output keeps the STRIPE. Smallest-area
order does the opposite: the short bars have less area than the
tall stripe, so they draw on top and overwrite the stripe. Twelve
cells wrong.

THE DIAGNOSIS

Smallest-area-on-top and earliest-legend-on-top are
INDISTINGUISHABLE on the training data. Whenever one rectangle
nests inside another, the inner rectangle is both the smaller one
AND the earlier legend entry, so the two laws predict the same
z-order on every training pair. They diverge only when rectangles
cross, and no training pair crosses. The test does.

The legend is the author's own explicit declaration of z-order:
its rows are listed top to bottom, and the earliest row draws on
top. That first-class signal is more trustworthy than the
geometric proxy of area, which merely happened to agree with it
in-distribution.

Confirming the fix by hand: the test legend maps, in order,
2->2, 5->9, 3->9, 1->4, 4->6. Color 2 is the first legend entry,
so the color-2 stripe draws on top of every bar and wins all
twelve crossing cells - exactly the answer. Color 4 is the last
legend entry, so its own two-column stripe (at columns 4 and 5)
still yields its crossings to the earlier bars, which is also
exactly the answer. One ordering law reproduces both behaviors.

THE REPAIR

The change is confined to arc2_transform(bbox_fill)/3 and its
helper arc2_bxf_fill_for_/4. The list of rectangle records was
sorted ascending by bounding-box area; it is now sorted ascending
by LEGEND INDEX - the position of each K-to-V mapping in the
legend, read with nth0. The fill lookup returns the first
matching rectangle in that order, so the earliest legend entry is
the topmost. Density (used to decide whether a sparse box repaints
its markers) is still computed from the bounding box exactly as
before; only the stacking key changed.

VERIFICATION

Every training pair still passes, because legend order equals area
order whenever rectangles nest - which is every training case. The
test now matches exactly: the twelve wrong cells are corrected and
no cell regresses. The full 120-task regression run confirms the
net effect is precisely plus one: task 9385bd28 moves from the
fail list into the pass list and nothing else changes. Induction
still returns bbox_fill for 9385bd28, and no other task's
induction result is altered.

A lesson of independent interest: an inductive tiebreaker can be a
COINCIDENCE of the training distribution. Two different laws that
agree on every visible example are not thereby the same law, and a
held-out configuration that pulls them apart is precisely what a
benchmark is for. When a rule passes every training pair yet
misses the test, suspect a tiebreaker on which two candidate laws
happen to agree in-distribution, and prefer the FIRST-CLASS signal
the puzzle actually provides - here, the legend's own ordering -
over any geometric proxy that merely correlates with it. A repair
wave is the cheapest summit step: a near-miss solver one ordering
away from correct becomes correct with a single sort key.
