The wave adds a rule that reads a grid as a physical apparatus: a stack
of walls and balls connected by a precise mechanical law of markers,
holes, and reflections.

TASK DESCRIPTION

The grid is a stack of alternating rows. Even rows are horizontal walls
drawn mostly in color 8, with the leftmost and rightmost cells in color
3, and occasional 0-holes punched through the wall. Odd rows are gap
rows drawn in 0, holding one or more 7-7-7 orange triplets - three
consecutive orange cells.

In the output:

  Every orange triplet is cross-painted: the center cell turns color 6,
  and the two flanking cells stay color 8. A single color-6 marker
  appears in the wall row directly above the triplet center and in the
  wall row directly below it.

  Holes in the wall rows and triplets near the side borders spawn pairs
  of green (color 3) marker cells.

  Some of those green pairs then reflect to the opposite border.

  The bottom wall row's corner cells are handled by a separate rule.

THE RULE

Part A - Cross-paint and vertical markers. For each 7-7-7 triplet: set
  the center cell to 6, the two flank cells to 8. Place a single 6 in
  the wall row directly above the triplet center and a single 6 in the
  wall row directly below it.

Part B - Wall-hole greening. For each interior 0-hole in a wall row:
  paint the hole's interior-facing neighbor green (color 3), and paint
  the nearer side border green. If the hole is within three columns of
  a side wall, also paint the far border green.

Part C - Near-border triplets. For each triplet whose left edge lies
  within three columns of the left wall: paint a two-cell green pair at
  columns 0 and 1 on the wall row just below that triplet. Similarly,
  a triplet whose right edge lies within three columns of the right wall
  paints a two-cell green pair at the rightmost two columns of that wall
  row.

Part D - Reflection. A green pair placed by Part C reflects to the
  opposite border two wall rows above, but ONLY if it is the sole green
  pair on that wall row. If the wall row sits in the lower half of the
  grid, a second reflection appears four wall rows below. If two near
  triplets share the same wall row, the pairs cancel each other and
  neither reflects.

Part E - Bottom wall corners. When the bottom row is a wall row: green
  the left corner if a near triplet (within three columns of the left
  wall) sits on the gap row just above, or if the left half of the
  bottom wall has no holes at all. Green the right corner under the
  same symmetric condition.

THE DISCRIMINATING OBSERVATION

The reflection cancellation in Part D is the key. On the surface both
training pairs seem to differ only in the positions of the triplets and
holes, but the test grid exercises a wall row that receives green pairs
from two near triplets simultaneously. When two near pairs share a wall
row, neither reflects. When exactly one near pair occupies a wall row,
it reflects. This one-vs-two condition - detected by counting near
pairs per wall row - is what divides the cases and produces the correct
pattern of green markers on the opposite border.
