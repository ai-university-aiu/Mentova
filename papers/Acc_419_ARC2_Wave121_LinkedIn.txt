Task 2b83f449 draws a grid as a stack of alternating wall rows and gap rows. The gap rows hold 7-7-7 orange triplets. The output marks each triplet, colors holes in the walls with green markers, and reflects some of those markers to the opposite border.

The glass-box rule I call wall_cross:

Part A: cross-paint each 7-7-7 triplet - center turns 6, flanks stay 8 - and place a single color-6 marker in the wall rows directly above and below the triplet center.

Part B: each 0-hole in a wall row spawns a green marker at the hole's interior-facing neighbor and at the nearer side border; a hole close to a side wall also greens the far border.

Part C: a triplet within three columns of a side wall spawns a two-cell green pair at that border on the wall row just below it.

Part D: a green pair placed by Part C reflects to the opposite border two wall rows above - but only if it is the sole green pair on that wall row. If two near triplets share a wall row their pairs cancel and neither reflects. The wall row in the lower half also reflects four rows below.

Part E: the bottom wall row's corner cells are greened based on whether a nearby triplet feeds them or the side has no holes.

The discriminating detail is the cancellation in Part D. Two near pairs on the same row cancel; one near pair on its own row reflects. That count-per-row condition was invisible in the training pairs and only revealed itself in the held-out test.
