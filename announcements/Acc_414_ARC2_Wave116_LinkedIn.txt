Accomplishment 414: ARC-AGI-2 Wave 116 - Concentric Rings Repair: Chebyshev Distance For True Nested Rectangles

PrologAI solves ARC-AGI-2 task 13e47133 with a one-line repair of the
existing glass-box rule concentric_rings (WP-374, Layer 349), raising
the benchmark score from 112/120 = 93.33% to 113/120 = 94.17%.

This one is a detective story about a single number being off by one.

The task: a background field is walled by a divider color into
regions. Each region fills with concentric rectangular rings, like the
contour lines of a hill, and small single-cell markers say which color
each ring should be. In the training pairs the divider draws one plain
rectangle. In the test it draws several nested boxes, one inside
another.

Our solver already reproduced BOTH training pairs of this task
exactly - and yet it missed the held-out test by twenty-nine cells,
every one of them a single color off, exactly one ring in the wrong
direction. When a rule nails training but slips on the test by a
uniform one-ring shift, the color logic is not the suspect. The
geometry is.

The cause was the distance metric. The ring index was measured with
four-connected steps - up, down, left, right - which is Manhattan
distance. For a plain rectangle that is perfectly correct: distance
from the four edges is exactly the concentric-ring index, so both
training rectangles came out right. But where two nested boxes meet,
the region has a concave corner, and Manhattan distance cannot cut
across a corner. It walks around and arrives one step late, over-
counting the ring by one along the diagonal - the twenty-nine cells.

The fix is one line: let the distance propagate with eight-connected
steps, which is Chebyshev distance. Chebyshev distance keeps the ring
index constant along a box corner, so the rings become true concentric
rectangles even around concave corners. All twenty-nine cells snap
into place, both training pairs still reproduce exactly, and the full
120-task benchmark shows the other 119 tasks unchanged. A clean plus
one with zero regression.

The lesson: a rule can pass every training example and still hide a
wrong metric, because the easy training geometry lets two different
metrics agree. Manhattan and Chebyshev boundary-distance are identical
on a plain rectangle and diverge only at a concave corner. Concentric
rectangles are a Chebyshev object, not a Manhattan one. Pure
induction. No pretraining. Every rule is named and glass-box.
