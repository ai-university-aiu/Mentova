Accomplishment 383: ARC-AGI-2 Wave 85 - Dial Rotor: Detached Dial Pieces Command Quarter-Turn Rotations of Ring and Core

PrologAI solves ARC-AGI-2 task 6ffbe589 with a new glass-box rule
called dial_rotor (WP-343, Layer 318), raising the benchmark score
from 81/120 = 67.50% to 82/120 = 68.33%.

Each grid holds one main figure - a framed square wrapped in a
dotted ring - plus a few small straight pieces lying far away. The
figure is cropped together with everything near it, and the crop
splits into a ring zone and a core block: the ring color is the
unique color on the crop's outermost layer, and the ring thickness
is how deep you must go before any other color appears. Then the
far-away pieces speak: each one is a dial. A straight run of color
C with length L commands "rotate color C's layer by L quarter turns
clockwise." The ring zone spins by the ring color's dial, and the
core block spins by the largest dial among its own colors.

The key insight was that the palette does not pick the rotation.
The second training grid and the hidden test share the exact same
colors - ring 5, core 3 and 4 - yet one needs a quarter turn of the
core and the other a half turn. The specks of debris that looked
like noise are the actual instruction stream: a "33" domino means
half turn for the 3s, a lone "4" means quarter turn for the 4s, and
the test's five-cell bar of ring color means five quarter turns -
one full revolution plus a quarter, proving the dials are read
modulo 4 like the hands of a clock.

The rule has no learned parameters. The figure, the dials, the ring
color, the ring thickness, and both rotation amounts are all read
off the input grid at answer time, and induction is pure
verification of all three training pairs. Pure induction. No
pretraining. Every rule is named and glass-box.
