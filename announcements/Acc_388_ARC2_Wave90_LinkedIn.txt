Accomplishment 388: ARC-AGI-2 Wave 90 - Compartment Tally: A Census of Subdivided Boxes

PrologAI solves ARC-AGI-2 task 89565ca0 with a new glass-box rule
called compartment_tally (WP-348, Layer 323), raising the benchmark
score from 86/120 = 71.67% to 87/120 = 72.50%.

Each grid holds one scattered noise color plus three to five box
colors on a majority background. Every box color draws one
rectangular outline, subdivided by internal partition lines into
table-like compartments - one to six per box. The boxes nest,
overlap, and cross each other's lines, and the noise cells land
everywhere, including on top of the box lines themselves. The
answer is a tiny census grid: one row per box, sorted by
compartment count, each row painting the box color once per
compartment and padding with the noise color.

The key insight is that the compartment count is structural, not
decorative. The rule finds the noise color as the one whose cells
shatter into the most fragments, then counts each box's
compartments by flood-filling the box interior. The walls are the
box's own cells plus a guarded single-cell repair: a non-background
cell directly flanked by two box-colored cells vertically or
horizontally is a partition cell that noise or a crossing box
overwrote, and is restored. The background guard matters as much as
the repair - without it, genuine single-row and single-column
compartments get walled off and vanish from the census; without the
repair, the flood leaks through every damaged partition. One box in
the training data hides a third compartment below an internal
partition that first looks like stray decoration; the flood fill
finds it without any special case.

The rule has no learned parameters. The background, the noise
color, the boxes, the repaired walls, and the compartment counts
are all read off the input grid at answer time, and induction is
pure verification of all three training pairs. Pure induction. No
pretraining. Every rule is named and glass-box.
