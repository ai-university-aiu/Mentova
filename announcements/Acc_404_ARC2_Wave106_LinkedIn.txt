Accomplishment 404: ARC-AGI-2 Wave 106 - Staple Gravity: Settling a Scaffold of Interlocked Pieces

PrologAI solves ARC-AGI-2 task b6f77b65 with a new glass-box rule
called staple_gravity (WP-364, Layer 339), raising the benchmark
score from 102/120 = 85.00% to 103/120 = 85.83%.

Each input of this task shows a corner cell naming a color, plus a
standing scaffold of colored pieces: vertical poles, horizontal
bars, and single wedged cells. Delete the named pieces and the
survivors settle under gravity - each rigid piece falling straight
down until something holds it again. When no piece carries the
named color, nothing happens at all.

The trap is that the scaffold hides its own geometry. This task
had been shelved twice under wrong readings - first as a template
dictionary, then as gravity with unexplained hidden shapes. The
insight that opened it is that the scene is a STATICS problem: the
input is stable, so every piece must already have a reason not to
fall. Listing those reasons reveals the mechanics. Bars hang
pinched between two poles and drop the moment one side vanishes.
Poles stand impaled one cell deep inside the bars they rest on -
which is why one piece shows seven cells in an input and eight in
its output: the eighth was hidden inside the bar all along. A
piece that appears to flip upside down between input and output
dissolves into a pole plus a separate one-cell piece wedged
against it. And because support is computed as a fixpoint growing
up from the floor, a falling piece can catch nothing on the way
down - pieces land in exactly the order the training pairs show.

The complete rule was written in plain English covering every grid
before any code, every fall distance in all four settling pairs
was derived by hand from the support rules, a Python prototype
confirmed all six grids on its first run, and the integrated
Prolog rule passed the single-task test and the full regression on
its first compile. Pure induction. No pretraining. Every rule is
named and glass-box.
