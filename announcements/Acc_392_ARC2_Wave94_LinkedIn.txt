Accomplishment 392: ARC-AGI-2 Wave 94 - Seam Weld: Assembling Pieces by Duplicated Connector Stubs

PrologAI solves ARC-AGI-2 task 4e34c42c with a new glass-box rule
called seam_weld (WP-352, Layer 327), raising the benchmark score
from 90/120 = 75.00% to 91/120 = 75.83%.

Each input of this task scatters several multi-color pieces on a
plain background. Look closely and you find that pairs of pieces
carry the same small pattern drawn twice - a five-cell column here
and the identical five-cell column there, a checkerboard corner, a
short bar, even just two lonely marker cells. These duplicated
patterns are connector stubs, and the answer is the single
assembly you get by welding every piece onto its partner so the
twin stubs sit exactly on top of each other.

The catch is that welding by maximum overlap alone fails. A big
piece can brush against another piece and agree on a few cells
purely by accident, and a greedy solver happily glues them
together the wrong way. The property that separates a true weld
from a false one is totality: in a true weld, a complete
same-color cluster of one piece lands exactly on a complete
same-color cluster of the other - the whole stub, not a part of
it. And since the smallest genuine connector in this task is just
two marker cells, no size threshold can stand in for that
condition.

The rule reads the pieces off the grid, inventories their
same-color clusters, seeds a canvas with the largest piece, and
welds the rest on one by one - every landed cell must agree
exactly, and one whole stub must cover one whole canvas stub. The
training scenes weld into flat horizontal chains; the test scene
welds six pieces into a two-dimensional tree taller and wider than
anything the training pairs show. The union bounding box of the
finished weld is the answer, cell for cell.

The complete rule was written in plain English covering every grid
before any code, and the integrated rule passed the single-task
test and the full regression on the first compile. Pure induction.
No pretraining. Every rule is named and glass-box.
