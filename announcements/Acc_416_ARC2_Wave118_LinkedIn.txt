Accomplishment 416: ARC-AGI-2 Wave 118 - Sequence Extrapolate: Reading A Growing Family Of Shapes And Drawing The Next One

PrologAI solves ARC-AGI-2 task 20a9e565 with a new glass-box rule,
sequence_extrapolate (WP-376, Layer 351), raising the benchmark score
from 114/120 = 95.00% to 115/120 = 95.83%.

This one reads a grid as a SEQUENCE. A short line of shapes grows in
size, step by step, and the answer is the next shape in the series.

The task: each grid carries two features. The first is a caliper - two
tiny L-trominoes in color 5, one at a top-left corner and one at a
bottom-right corner. The rectangle that just holds both of them names
the height and width of the answer, and nothing else. The second is a
run of same-family shapes that grow from one member to the next, each
drawn in a color that cycles through a short list.

The five grids use five different families. A comb with a spine and two
teeth. A ladder whose rows alternate full rungs and side rails. A band
of little triangular peaks repeated across. A two-cell-wide braid of
colored rings nested around a center. Each grows in its own way, and
the answer is the next member of whichever family the grid shows -
generated at full size, then cropped to the caliper rectangle and
painted in the next color of the cycle.

The clean idea here is the split between SIZE and CONTENT. The caliper
says how big; the sequence says what it looks like. Because those two
jobs are kept apart, one rule and one cropping step serve four families
that share nothing else. The way the rule picks which family it is
looking at is the growth vector between members - grow by one in both
directions and it is a comb, grow taller only and it is a ladder or a
braid depending on how many colors a member uses, grow wider only and
it is a band of peaks. Reading the growth rather than matching a fixed
template is what lets the rule recognize a family on a grid it has
never seen. The held-out test is the peaks family, extrapolated to the
caliper width and cropped to two rows.

One practical note: extracting the shapes is the only costly step, so
the rule only bothers when a grid actually holds the two color-5
caliper trominoes. That gate rejects every other task after counting a
few cells, and the full 120-task benchmark confirms zero regression.

Pure induction. No pretraining. Every rule is named and glass-box.
