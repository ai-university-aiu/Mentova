Accomplishment 408: ARC-AGI-2 Wave 110 - Snake Dock: Two-Colored Snakes, Container Pockets, and the Parity Color Law

PrologAI solves ARC-AGI-2 task a25697e4 with a new glass-box rule
called snake_dock (WP-368, Layer 343), raising the benchmark
score from 106/120 = 88.33% to 107/120 = 89.17%.

Each scene of this task holds two kinds of objects on a
majority-color background. A SNAKE is a single connected piece
made of exactly two single-color segments joined orthogonally. A
CONTAINER is one or two nearby walls of a single color whose
bounding box holds a pocket of background cells - the socket -
open through a mouth. In the output every snake has left its
place and docked rigidly into its container: one segment fills
the socket exactly, matched under one of the eight grid
isometries, and the other segment trails outside through the
mouth. The test grid raises the stakes with two snakes and two
containers at once, paired uniquely by segment size.

The delicate part is the coloring. Two training grids share the
same snake pixel for pixel, and only the container is mirrored -
yet the two outputs paint the socket in different colors. The
only property that predicts the paint, across all five docking
events of the task, is the parity of the docking isometry. After
a proper rotation, each segment keeps its own color. After a
reflection, the two segment colors are exchanged - a snake that
must be flipped over to fit shows its underside, like a physical
tile turned face-down. Five events, five correct predictions:
transpose and vertical flip swap the colors, translation and
both quarter turns keep them.

The wave's lesson: when identical inputs produce differently
colored outputs, the discriminator hides in the part of the
scene that DID change. Treat rigid placements as isometry plus
translation, and test the isometry's parity as a first-class
feature - reflections can carry meaning.

The complete rule was written in plain English covering every
grid before any code, all five docking events were verified cell
by cell by hand, a Python prototype confirmed the algorithm on
its first run, and the integrated Prolog rule passed the
single-task test and the full regression on its first compile.
Pure induction. No pretraining. Every rule is named and
glass-box.
