Accomplishment 400: ARC-AGI-2 Wave 102 - Ray Scatter: Border Rays Blasting a Blob into the Corners

PrologAI solves ARC-AGI-2 task 4a21e3da with a new glass-box rule
called ray_scatter (WP-360, Layer 335), raising the benchmark
score from 98/120 = 81.67% to 99/120 = 82.50%.

Each input of this task shows one connected blob of a single color
floating near the middle of the grid, and one or two lone marker
dots resting on the border. In the output the blob has been blasted
apart. Every marker fires a ray straight into the grid along its
own row or column, reaching exactly as far as the farthest blob
cell on that line; the background along the ray takes the marker
color, and the blob cells the ray passes through stay welded in
place. Everything else flies: the blob's side pieces translate
rigidly into the corners of the grid, each landing flush against
two walls with its shape preserved cell for cell.

The geometry has two twists. Across a ray, a piece flees the line
to the wall on its own side - but along a single ray, both pieces
fly toward the marker's end of the grid, not away from it. And in
the two-ray training pair, one piece simply vanishes: the markers
sit on the top and right edges, and while the top-left, top-right,
and bottom-right pieces land in corners touching a marked edge,
the bottom-left corner touches neither - so its piece is swallowed
whole. That deletion test is the observation that cracks the task.

The hand trace earned its keep this wave. A first reading of the
second training pair invented a phantom missing cell, which bred
three wrong deletion hypotheses before a careful re-print of the
expected output showed the piece intact all along. The final rule
needs no cell-level deletions at all - just rays, anchored line
cells, rigid corner translations, and the one whole-piece rule.

The complete rule was written in plain English covering every grid
before any code, confirmed by a full cell-level diff and a Python
prototype reproducing all four grids exactly, and the integrated
Prolog rule passed the single-task test and the full regression on
its first compile. Pure induction. No pretraining. Every rule is
named and glass-box.
