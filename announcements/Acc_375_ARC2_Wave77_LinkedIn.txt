Accomplishment 375: ARC-AGI-2 Wave 77 - Palette Jigsaw Corner-Recolored Square Assembly

PrologAI solves ARC-AGI-2 task f560132c with a new glass-box rule called
palette_jigsaw (WP-335, Layer 310), raising the benchmark score from
73/120 = 60.83% to 74/120 = 61.67%.

The input grid scatters four pieces over the background: three
single-color connected shapes and one key piece whose body embeds a
2-by-2 block of four foreign palette colors in place of four of its own
cells. The total number of piece cells is always a perfect square. The
output is the assembled square: every piece rotated by quarter turns,
the rotated pieces interlocking with no gaps and no overlaps, the key
piece covering the top-left corner, and every piece repainted with the
palette color of the output corner it covers - the key takes the
palette's top-left color, and the pieces on the other three corners
take the colors of their corners.

The key insight is that the palette carrier anchors the corner. Without
any anchoring constraint each grid admits exactly four distinct-corner
tilings - the four global rotations of one and the same assembly - and
only one of them recolors correctly. Pinning the key piece to the
top-left corner, the same corner where its own palette reading begins,
collapses every grid, training and test alike, to exactly one tiling,
confirmed by exhaustive enumeration. This generalizes the Wave 76
puzzle_fit anchor from a single colored stud to a role-bearing piece,
and the entire Wave 76 exact-cover engine is reused without
modification.

The engine extracts the pieces as 8-connected mixed-color components,
reads the 2-by-2 palette out of the key piece, converts the pieces to
indexed shape masks, derives the square's side from the total cell
count, runs the scanline-anchored exact-cover search with quarter-turn
rotations, and repaints each placed piece by the corner it covers. The
rule carries no learned parameters: background, pieces, palette, square
size, rotations, and the anchored corner are all derived from the input
grid itself. Both training pairs and the hidden test case pass. Pure
induction. No pretraining. Every rule is named and glass-box.
