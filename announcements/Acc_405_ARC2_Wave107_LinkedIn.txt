Accomplishment 405: ARC-AGI-2 Wave 107 - Ray Merge: Leaking Rings and the Collision Algebra of Rays

PrologAI solves ARC-AGI-2 task e12f9a14 with a new glass-box rule
called ray_merge (WP-365, Layer 340), raising the benchmark score
from 103/120 = 85.83% to 104/120 = 86.67%.

Each input of this task shows small boxes: one-cell rectangular
rings, each drawn around a filled interior of its own color. The
rings are leaky. Missing outline cells are gaps, and every gap
leaks a ray of the box's interior color - perpendicular from an
edge gap, diagonally outward from a corner gap - running straight
to the grid border.

Unless two rays are about to collide. Then neither yields: both
stop one cell short of the crossing and continue side by side
along the vector sum of their courses. Two facing diagonals
straighten into a parallel vertical pair. A perpendicular meeting
bends both rays onto the shared diagonal. A diagonal converging on
a straight ray sends both onto a slope-two staircase. And a pair
that has already merged collides later as a single ray - its
course counts once in the next sum - which is why a three-ray
pileup travels diagonally while a fresh two-ray convergence climbs
a staircase. Recovering this collision algebra from the added
cells of the training pairs is what cracked the task.

The wave also produced a finding of independent interest: one
training demonstration omits exactly one ray. A corner gap that
fires in one pair stays dark in its mirror-image twin, and the
official ARC-AGI-2 repository data confirms the omission cell for
cell - the demonstrations themselves can carry an authoring slip.
The induction verifier therefore tolerates at most one training
pair deviating in at most eight extra-paint-only cells, while the
test pair matches the full mechanics exactly.

The complete rule was written in plain English covering every grid
before any code, every added cell in every pair was assigned to
its ray by hand, a Python prototype confirmed the mechanics on its
first run, and the integrated Prolog rule passed the single-task
test and the full regression on its first compile. Pure induction.
No pretraining. Every rule is named and glass-box.
