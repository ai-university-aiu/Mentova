Accomplishment 396: ARC-AGI-2 Wave 98 - Twin Beam: Firing Lamp Probes from Twin-Bar Separators

PrologAI solves ARC-AGI-2 task 9bbf930d with a new glass-box rule
called twin_beam (WP-356, Layer 331), raising the benchmark score
from 94/120 = 78.33% to 95/120 = 79.17%.

Each input of this task is a ladder: even rows carry a colored
horizontal bar plus pipe-like decorations to the right, odd rows
are empty separator corridors, and every row opens with a small 6
lamp in column zero. Wherever two consecutive bars share one
color, the separator between them loses its lamp - and a 6 appears
somewhere else entirely: at the far end of the row, deep inside
the pipe decorations, or pressed against a distant wall.

The lamp is a probe. It leaves column zero heading right, glides
through the empty corridors, and turns wherever the pipes force
it: blocked ahead with exactly one open side, it turns there and
keeps going; blocked at a two-way junction or a dead end, it rests
and lights up where it stands. One probe in the test grid crosses
twenty rows and turns twice before finding its resting place.

The trick that cracks the task: two junctions can be locally
identical in every surrounding cell, yet one probe must rest while
the other must turn left. The difference is not in the walls - it
is in the lamps. A probe refuses to turn left along a row that
still holds its own lamp, and gladly turns left along a row whose
lamp has already fired. The vacated column-zero lamps are not
cosmetic; they are state that later probes consult. No purely
local turning rule solves this task.

The complete rule was written in plain English covering every grid
before any code, a Python prototype confirmed it - exposing one
ordering subtlety, that junction arity must be tested before the
lamp guard is applied - and the integrated Prolog rule passed the
single-task test and the full regression on the first compile.
Pure induction. No pretraining. Every rule is named and glass-box.
