Accomplishment 402: ARC-AGI-2 Wave 104 - Box Tally: Hollow Frames Counting Their Scattered Siblings

PrologAI solves ARC-AGI-2 task 8f215267 with a new glass-box rule
called box_tally (WP-362, Layer 337), raising the benchmark score
from 100/120 = 83.33% to 101/120 = 84.17%.

Each input of this task shows a stack of hollow rectangular boxes
along the left side - one-cell-thick frames, each in its own color,
each with a clean background interior - while the rest of the grid
is sprinkled with small scattered shapes of assorted colors:
dominoes, squares, blocks, plus signs, and diamonds.

In the output the scatter is gone. Every box stands exactly where
it stood, except for a short row of dots printed on its middle
interior row, right-aligned and spaced two cells apart. Each box
has answered a counting question: how many separate pieces of my
color lie scattered out there? Four pieces, four dots. One piece,
one dot. No pieces, no dots. Colors with no box of their own
simply vanish.

The quiet trap is what exactly gets counted. In the first training
pair the color-8 scatter totals ten cells spread over four pieces
of four different shapes - and the box prints exactly four dots.
Cells, areas, and shapes all disagree; only the count of separate
connected pieces fits every box in every grid. The task had been
shelved for seven waves under a stale surface reading about
instruction patches and stripe counts; a fresh dump of the actual
grids dissolved it in minutes. There is no lookup - each framed
box is simply a counter.

The complete rule was written in plain English covering every grid
before any code, with every scatter component counted and every
dot position checked by hand, and the integrated Prolog rule
passed the single-task test and the full regression on its first
compile. Pure induction. No pretraining. Every rule is named and
glass-box.
