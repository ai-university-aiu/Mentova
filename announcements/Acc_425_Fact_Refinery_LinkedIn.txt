Acc_425: A Fact Refinery With a Minority Report Panel

There is a hard problem at the center of building an honest artificial mind.

Language models are the best readers ever built.  They are also fluent guessers that can sound completely sure while being completely wrong.

Mentova's whole identity is that it never bluffs.

So how do you use an army of brilliant, unreliable readers to grow a mind that refuses to guess?

Today's milestone is the answer: a fact refinery.

Raw text goes in one end.  Clean, checked, sourced candidate facts come out the other.

And not one fluent guess is trusted along the way.

Here is how.

A cheap local model reads a passage and proposes facts in a strict shape — subject, relation, object, the exact source sentence, and a citation.

A stronger model qualifies each one: is it the kind of fact you can perceive, like a ball being round, or the kind a trusted record attests, like the Peace of Westphalia being signed in 1648?

Then comes the panel.

Several genuinely different models — different makers, different training — vote on each fact.  Blind, so they cannot echo one another.  Normalized, so the same fact in different words counts as agreement.

Saying "I do not know" is an honest abstention, never a disagreement.

Unanimous facts ride a high-confidence lane.  Four of five gets a lighter human look.  A split goes to the Minority Report Bin — named for the film — where a person, or a stronger referee model, settles it.

And then the part I care about most.

Even a unanimous panel does not get the last word.

A plain program — ordinary rule-following code, on which no model gets a vote — checks that every word of the fact connects to concepts Mentova has already grounded, and that the citation is real and reachable.

In today's demonstration, the panel unanimously endorsed a fact about the moon.

The gate rejected it anyway, because Mentova has not grounded "moon" yet.

The models concurred.  Reality was still asked to concur separately.

Facts that survive enter the same review queue human mentors use.  Approved facts are grounded with their entire story attached — the record, the exact sentence, who voted which way, and the checks that passed.

Ask Mentova "why?" and it shows you all of it.

Twelve acceptance checks.  Twelve passes.

This is how the library on the shelf becomes knowledge in the head — one checked, sourced, reviewed fact at a time — without the mind ever learning to bluff.
