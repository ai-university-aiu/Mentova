Accomplishment 403: ARC-AGI-2 Wave 105 - Dot Crawler: A Shape Walking an Orthogonal Chain of Dots

PrologAI solves ARC-AGI-2 task 5545f144 with a new glass-box rule
called dot_crawler (WP-363, Layer 338), raising the benchmark score
from 101/120 = 84.17% to 102/120 = 85.00%.

Each input of this task shows a small shape with a single
protruding nose cell, plus a sprinkling of lone dots of the same
color. The training grids are split by separator columns into
panels - and the panels are not layers to be combined. They are
TIME: successive snapshots of one crawl. The dots are a route
whose consecutive stops always share a row or a column, the
crawler's nose rides exactly on top of each visited dot, and
visited dots vanish from later panels. The test grid drops the
separators entirely and shows a single frame.

The output is the final frame: a clean panel holding only the
crawler, docked nose-first on the last dot of the route, its body
rotated by the quarter turn that points the nose along the
direction of the final leg.

The quiet trap is that a route can be read from either end. In the
second training grid the shape presents two candidate noses, and
both thread complete chains through all four dots - exact reverses
of each other. Only the snapshot panels reveal which end the
crawler actually walks: in the second panel the true first stop is
already covered by the shape, while the false one still sits free.
The task had been shelved since Wave 90 under a wrong panel-overlay
reading; accounting for every single dot dissolved it.

The complete rule was written in plain English covering every grid
before any code, every chain was walked stop by stop by hand, a
Python prototype confirmed all four grids, and the integrated
Prolog rule passed the single-task test and the full regression on
its first compile. Pure induction. No pretraining. Every rule is
named and glass-box.
