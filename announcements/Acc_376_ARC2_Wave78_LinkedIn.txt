Accomplishment 376: ARC-AGI-2 Wave 78 - Gadget Assembly by Connector-Stud Pairing

PrologAI solves ARC-AGI-2 task cbebaa4b with a new glass-box rule
called gadget_assembly (WP-336, Layer 311), raising the benchmark
score from 74/120 = 61.67% to 75/120 = 62.50%.

The input grid scatters five to seven gadget pieces over the
background, each an 8-connected shape of one body color carrying
color-2 connector studs on its boundary. The output is the single
connected machine assembled by sliding the pieces together, without
rotation, so that every stud coincides exactly with its partner stud
on another piece. The tell-tale signature: the color-2 cell count
halves from input to output - 16 to 8, 16 to 8, and 24 to 12 across
the three grids - because paired studs merge into one cell, while
every other color keeps its exact cell count.

The engine reads each stud's outward facing from its unique body
neighbor, seeds the assembly with one piece, and runs a backtracking
search that repeatedly mates the first open stud with an
opposite-facing stud of an unplaced piece, translating the piece so
the two studs coincide. A placement is accepted only when every
colliding cell is a stud closing an open stud, and the search
succeeds when every piece is placed and no stud remains open.

The key insight is that the anchor is a graph-theoretic role. Wave 76
anchored its assembly on a colored stud and Wave 77 on a role-bearing
palette piece; Wave 78 anchors on the center of the connection graph.
The stud pairings form a graph - a simple path of 5, 5, and 7 pieces
across the three grids - and the piece with minimum eccentricity,
found by breadth-first search, keeps its exact input position while
the whole machine is drawn around it. The rule carries no learned
parameters: background, pieces, studs, facings, translations, the
connection graph, and the anchor are all derived from the input grid
itself. Both training pairs and the hidden test case pass, on the
first complete prototype run with zero bugs fixed. Pure induction. No
pretraining. Every rule is named and glass-box.
