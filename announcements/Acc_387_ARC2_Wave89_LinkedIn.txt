Accomplishment 387: ARC-AGI-2 Wave 89 - Wire Wrap: Re-Laying a Crumpled Wire Snugly Around a Marker

PrologAI solves ARC-AGI-2 task 88bcf3b4 with a new glass-box rule
called wire_wrap (WP-347, Layer 322), raising the benchmark score
from 85/120 = 70.83% to 86/120 = 71.67%.

Each grid holds one or more three-color systems on a uniform
background: a straight line segment, a crumpled meandering wire
attached to one endpoint of the line, and a separate marker shape -
a single cell, a short bar, a zigzag, or a staircase blob. The
answer keeps the line and the marker exactly where they are and
re-lays the wire as a clean path: it leaves the line end, runs
diagonally to the marker, bends snugly around the marker's near
face cell by cell, and departs on the mirrored diagonal, cut off at
the grid edge or when the wire's cells run out.

The key insight is that the wire's length is not always conserved.
Two of the five training pairs lose wire cells because the re-laid
path hits the grid edge before the length is spent, which rules out
every rigid-transform, rotation, and length-preserving reading of
the task. The wire is not moved - it is recomputed as pure
geometry: a diagonal approach clamped to the marker's near lane, a
hug that lays one cell beyond each marker row's or column's near
edge so zigzags and staircases are followed automatically, and a
45-degree departure with the sideways direction reversed. The test
case doubles the difficulty with two independent systems in one
scene - six foreground colors - and the solver pairs each line with
its wire by endpoint attachment and each system with its nearest
marker.

The rule has no learned parameters. The background, the lines, the
wires, the markers, the approach diagonal, the hug cells, and the
truncation length are all read off the input grid at answer time,
and induction is pure verification of all five training pairs. Pure
induction. No pretraining. Every rule is named and glass-box.
