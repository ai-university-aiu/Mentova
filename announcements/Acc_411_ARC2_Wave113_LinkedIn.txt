Accomplishment 411: ARC-AGI-2 Wave 113 - Bbox Fill Repair: When Two Laws Only Agree In-Distribution

PrologAI solves ARC-AGI-2 task 9385bd28 with a single-tiebreaker
repair of the glass-box bbox_fill rule (WP-371, Layer 346),
raising the benchmark score from 109/120 = 90.83% to
110/120 = 91.67%.

This was a repair wave. The rule already induced correctly on the
task - every training pair verified - yet its held-out answer was
wrong in exactly twelve cells. In this puzzle a small legend at
the bottom-left maps each marker color to a fill color, and each
color paints two L-tromino corners that define a rectangle drawn
solid in its fill color. Where rectangles overlap, a stacking
order decides which fill shows. All four training pairs NEST their
rectangles - a small one inside a larger one - and the rule
stacked them by bounding-box area, smallest on top, which passed
every training pair.

The held-out test does not nest - it CROSSES. A color-2 stripe two
columns wide runs the full height of the grid and passes through
three horizontal bars, none of which contains the other. The
correct answer keeps the stripe at the crossings, but
smallest-area order let the shorter bars win. Twelve cells wrong.

The diagnosis is the interesting part: smallest-area-on-top and
earliest-legend-on-top are INDISTINGUISHABLE whenever rectangles
nest, because the inner rectangle is both the smaller shape and
the earlier legend entry. The two laws diverge only when shapes
cross, and no training pair crosses. The legend's own top-to-bottom
order is the author's explicit z-order declaration - a first-class
signal, more trustworthy than the geometric proxy of area that
merely happened to agree with it in-distribution. Sorting the
rectangles by legend index instead of area corrects all twelve
cells, keeps every training pair passing, and regresses nothing
across the full 120-task benchmark.

The wave's lesson: an inductive tiebreaker can be a coincidence of
the training distribution. Two laws that agree on every visible
example are not thereby the same law, and a held-out configuration
that pulls them apart is exactly what a benchmark is for. When a
rule passes every training pair yet misses the test, suspect a
tiebreaker, and prefer the first-class signal the puzzle provides
over any proxy that merely correlates with it. Pure induction. No
pretraining. Every rule is named and glass-box.
