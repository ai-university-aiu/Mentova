MENTOVA KNOWLEDGE STORAGE POLICY

From Appendix Q of Teach_Mentova_To_Chat_v2 (2026-07-10).


THE ONE-BREATH SUMMARY

Mentova's knowledge is split into two very different kinds.

The understood notebook is the grounded Lattice of facts Mentova truly understands and can justify; it stays small and lives on the fast internal drive.

The look-it-up library is the large reference bodies, such as Wikipedia and the full California curriculum, which Mentova consults one page at a time; it lives on a larger, cheaper drive.

The rule: keep the understood notebook small and fast, keep the big library separate, and never confuse the library on the shelf with the knowledge in the head.


THE TWO KINDS OF KNOWLEDGE

The grounded Lattice is curated, verified, and tied through simpler words down to perception (direct grounding) or to a trusted record (testimonial grounding), and every fact in it can answer a "why?" question.

The reference library is raw text Mentova has not yet understood, held so it can be searched and read on demand, never asserted as fact - unread text asserted as fact is exactly the bluffing this project forbids.

Understanding grows as a frontier: selected pieces are promoted from the library into the grounded Lattice through the fact refinery (src/mentova/fact_refinery.pl), with panel checking, a plain-code honesty gate, and human review.

In code, the library tier is src/mentova/reference_library.pl: registered sources are streamed page by page and searched line by line, and citations of the shape source(SourceId, LineNo) are the currency of testimonial grounding.


APPROXIMATE DATA SIZES (MID 2026)

• Suggested Upper Merged Ontology (SUMO): about 20 megabytes.

• WordNet: about 50 megabytes as a database.

• ConceptNet: 9 to 10 gigabytes to download, 24 to 30 gigabytes loaded into PostgreSQL with indexes; rebuilding it needs at least 30 gigabytes of memory, so load the ready-made copy, never rebuild.

• California curriculum standards: tens to a few hundred megabytes as text.

• English Wikipedia, current articles only: about 25 gigabytes compressed, 20 to 25 gigabytes as extracted plain text; the 10-terabyte full revision history is never downloaded.

Working disk budget: roughly 30 to 40 gigabytes for ontologies plus ConceptNet, and another 100 to 200 gigabytes once Wikipedia and its search helpers are added.


WHY LARGE DATA STAYS OUT OF GIT

GitHub blocks single files over 100 megabytes, warns over 50, caps browser uploads at 25, and recommends repositories stay under 1 gigabyte.

Therefore ConceptNet, Wikipedia, and any full corpus never live in this repository; the code and tiny ontology files live in git, and large datasets are downloaded from their official sources at build time.

This policy is enforced by the repository's .gitignore, which excludes data/reference_library/, data/corpora/, data/refinery/, and the runtime data/chat_db/.


THE STORAGE DECISIONS TAKEN

• A two-terabyte external solid-state drive (not a spinning disk) will be added on a fast USB port for Wikipedia, ConceptNet, the curriculum, search helpers, and backups.

• For now, while the text-only chat is built and tested, everything stays on the internal drive, whose free space easily covers the ontologies and ConceptNet.

• When the big libraries are actually ingested, they and the backups move to the external solid-state drive.

• Wikipedia and the full curriculum are treated as the look-it-up library, promoted into understanding only through the refinery frontier.


THE SCALING TOOLKIT

• Store knowledge in tiers: memory for the hot grounded set, PostgreSQL on disk for the warm Lattice and reference tables, the external drive for the cold raw corpora.

• Use search rather than wholesale grounding for big text: full-text search and vector search find the one relevant passage on demand.

• Keep large data out of git; fetch it from official sources at build time.

• Stream, do not slurp: read giant files a piece at a time so they never have to fit in memory (reference_library.pl reads line by line by construction).

• Grow the hardware later as needed: ample solid-state disk now, more memory when in-memory work demands it, a larger database server when the public chat grows.
